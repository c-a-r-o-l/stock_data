StockGPT

A market-state retrieval & prediction engine — MarketBERT, not MarketGPT.

Encode the last 252 trading days of factor data into a dense 768-dim embedding of the current market state. Retrieve the 50 most similar historical regimes with Faiss to explain the call, and — on a separate, parallel path — calibrate P(top-decile 6-month return) with XGBoost trained on all historical embeddings.
window 252 trading days output 768-dim market state 28 daily features (incl. SYMBOL) encoder bidirectional, within-window projection num_factors → 768 runs on 32 GB CPU box

01 · System at a glance

One encoder, two parallel consumers

Everything downstream feeds off the same 768-dim embedding. Faiss and XGBoost are parallel paths, not a pipeline — XGBoost never trains on retrieval results, only on the full history.

02 · Decision ledger

Every call, and what it beat

Each architectural decision recorded blotter-style: the position taken (▲), the alternative closed out (▼), and the reasoning that survived the debate.

PositionRationale
▲ Transformer encoder over ▼ GPT decoder

The job is representation for retrieval, not text generation. An encoder reads the whole window with bidirectional attention and emits one fixed vector; a decoder would try to hallucinate tomorrow’s candles.

▲ Continuous embeddings over ▼ Discretized tokens

A linear projection (num_factors → 768) keeps exact values — 95.4 stays 95.4, not “RS_90_HIGH”. No arbitrary bin edges, smooth transitions preserved. Financial data is numbers, not words.

▲ 252-day window over ▼ 60-day window

One year captures ~4 quarterly updates, seasonal patterns and annual cycles. Sixty days sees a single quarterly print — not enough to read a fundamental trend.

▲ Faiss ∥ XGBoost (parallel) over ▼ Retrieval → train pipeline

Training on 50 retrieved neighbours is overfitting by construction. XGBoost learns from every historical embedding; Faiss exists so a human can see the precedent behind the number.

▲ Transformer over ▼ LSTM

No forgetting over long windows — attention can directly compare day 1 with day 252. Trains in parallel; positional encoding keeps time order.

▲ Raw ingredients over ▼ Pre-digested signals

Give SMA_20 and SMA_200; let the model learn the gap, slope and acceleration. Binary crossovers and fixed formulas like TTM = 2·STD − 1.5·ATR impose thresholds the model would learn better itself.

▲ Keep correlated features over ▼ Pre-solving multicollinearity

For trees, correlation is a problem; for attention, it’s context. The degree of divergence between correlated features is itself a signal. Only drop what is mathematically identical.

▲ Release-date alignment over ▼ Period-end alignment

Q1 GDP lands on the model’s tape at end of April, not January 1. Each economic release is its own dataset, merged by publication date — exactly what a live system would have known.

▲ Econ events as metadata over ▼ Econ events as features

Earnings, GDP, CPI and Fed decisions ride alongside embeddings to explain retrieved cases (“this precedent occurred during a hiking cycle”) — they are never fed to the encoder.

▲ Train from scratch over ▼ Pre-trained BERT weights

Hugging Face BERT read Wikipedia, not factor vectors. A small 6-layer encoder trained from scratch on decades of daily market states is the right-sized model — and it fits on a laptop.

04 · Data discipline

No leakage, ever

The rules that keep the backtest honest — alignment, forward-fill, and a strictly chronological split.

Same shape every day

Every trading day carries the same column count. Slower frequencies are forward-filled to daily:

weekly → 5d · monthly → 21d · quarterly → ~63d

Forward-fill is causal

It carries the last known value until new data arrives — only past values fill future days, exactly like a real-time feed. No bias introduced.

Merge by release date

Each economic release is a separate dataset joined on publication date, so every day holds only what was actually public at the time.

Bidirectional ≠ clairvoyant

The encoder attends left-and-right within days 1–252. It never sees day 253 or any future return during encoding.

05 · Alpha map

Where the excess return comes from

Four distinct sources, each tied to specific features — not a single magic signal.

α·1 — Microstructure

Main-force structure

Trend_PV quantifies the full attack → consolidate → attack cycle of institutional pushing, where most treat “main force” as just big orders.

Trend_PV · PV_BULL_* → hold strength, avoid shakeouts
α·2 — Asymmetric

Exhaustion & acceleration

Trigger and Itrend read the second derivative of trend. Close > Trigger > Itrend confirms continuation; MACD_C above a rising MACD_H is overbought expansion — bullish, not a warning.

Trigger · Itrend · MACD_C>MACD_H → enter early, exit before the drop
α·3 — Regime-dependent

Context-aware weights

Fixed-weight models break across regimes. Macro context lets attention learn that PV_BULL works in broad, low-VIX tape and defensive factors work in crises.

SP_Above_200 · VIX structure · Fosback → adaptive weighting
α·4 — Data

Structured insight in

Inputs are engineered insights — trend efficiency, step duration, fragility — not raw OHLCV. Layer 1 sees trend quality instead of spending ten layers learning it.

PFE · PV_BULL_STEP · Fosback → lighter learning burden
ALPHA EQUATION α = (Main-force attack structure × Trend acceleration) + (Overbought momentum expansion × Market-breadth filter) − (Noise in panic environments)

07 · Hard questions

The interview-grade FAQ

The challenges this design has to survive — with the answers that hold up.

Isn’t a bidirectional encoder looking at the future?
No. “Bidirectional” means attention flows left-to-right and right-to-left within the historical window (days 1–252). The encoder never sees day 253 or any future return. Bidirectionality is about fully connecting the past, not peeking forward.
Doesn’t the encoder lose information?
Yes — deliberately. It’s lossy compression: 3,000+ raw, noisy, highly correlated numbers become 768 meaningful ones. The loss falls on redundancy and noise; the underlying market state is what survives. That’s the point.
Why not feed a raw flat vector straight into XGBoost?
You can, but XGBoost treats 252 × 28 numbers as independent features — it has no idea that feature #700 is the same factor as feature #728 one day later. The encoder’s positional encoding and self-attention give the model the temporal structure trees can’t represent.
Why keep both Faiss and XGBoost?
Different jobs. Faiss = interpretability: auditable precedents that build trust. XGBoost = accuracy: a calibrated probability learned from all data. They are parallel — the classifier is never trained on retrieval output.
Why drop SPY_O / SPY_H / SPY_L but keep SPY_C?
SPY_C (stock close / SPY close) captures the core stock-vs-market relationship. Open, high and low of the ratio add no independent information beyond the close for this purpose — three features cut, nothing lost.
How do daily, weekly, monthly and quarterly data coexist?
Forward-fill everything to daily so each day is one fixed-length vector. The encoder doesn’t care about native frequencies — and because the fill only carries last known values forward, it mirrors a live feed with no lookahead.
What does this need to run?
A 32 GB CPU machine is more than enough. The model uses under 2 GB and trains in roughly 2–6 hours on CPU. No GPU required at this scale.
What exactly does XGBoost predict?
A binary probability: P(this window’s forward return lands in the top decile). It is not a ranking model — but sorting predicted probabilities across a universe of stocks yields a ranking for free.

08 · Open items

What makes it bulletproof

The gaps a sharp interviewer will probe first — close these before calling the project done.

Backtesting framework

backtrader or vectorbt: buy above threshold, hold 6 months. Report equity curve, Sharpe, max drawdown — benchmarked against buy & hold. Without this, every probability is a castle in the air.

CRITICAL

Baseline comparison

Logistic regression, random forest, and factor-only XGBoost. The embedding has to earn its place — target an AUC lift of 5+ points over the best baseline.

CRITICAL

Wire in FRED macro & breadth

Growth/value ratios, the VIX term structure, and breadth families for SPY / QQQ / RSL / NYSE — the regime context that α·3 depends on.

NEXT

Regime-change handling

Periodic index rebuilds (e.g. weekly) and optional time-decayed retrieval so precedents stay relevant when market structure shifts.
