1. PURPOSE & METHODOLOGY
================================================================================

This report identifies the most relevant macroeconomic indicators from the
Federal Reserve Economic Data (FRED) database for predicting US stock market
regimes. The end goal is to feed these indicators into the StockGPT "Market
Language" token system, where each indicator's state is converted into discrete
tokens (e.g., PMI_CONTRACTION, YIELD_CURVE_INVERTED, NET_LIQ_CONTRACTING).

Selection Criteria:
- LEADING > lagging: indicators that turn BEFORE the economy/market turn
- Multi-decade data: minimum 20+ years, most have 50-80+ years
- Market relevance: empirically linked to S&P 500 direction or volatility
- Non-redundant: avoids duplication with existing 22 series in ATM_ID.py
- Broad coverage: employment, inflation, PMI, money flow, FX, commodities,
  bonds, credit, sentiment, carry trade, international flows

Methodology:
1. Reviewed The Conference Board Leading Economic Index (LEI) 10 components
2. Reviewed Federal Reserve research (Estrella & Mishkin yield curve studies,
   Chicago Fed NFCI, St. Louis Fed Financial Stress Index)
3. Reviewed academic literature on S&P 500 predictability (Cernohorsky 2016,
   Lindenau & Demetrescu 2025, Shi et al. 2025)
4. Reviewed macro trader frameworks (Net Liquidity = WALCL - TGA - RRP)
5. Cross-referenced with existing ATM_ID.py to avoid duplication

2. INDICATOR CATALOG

-------------------------------
CATEGORY A: MONEY FLOW / LIQUIDITY / FED BALANCE SHEET (~25 series)
-------------------------------

A1. FED BALANCE SHEET & NET LIQUIDITY (8 series)

A1  WALCL              Fed Total Assets (Wednesday Level, W, 2002+)
    THE liquidity gauge. Total size of Fed balance sheet. Expanding during QE
    (bullish for risk assets), shrinking during QT (bearish). Peaked ~$9T
    post-COVID March 2020.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/WALCL

A2  WTREGEN            Treasury General Account - TGA (Week Avg, W, 2002+)
    Treasury's checking account at the Fed. TGA RISES when taxes are collected
    and debt is issued = liquidity DRAIN from banking system. TGA FALLS when
    Treasury spends = liquidity INJECTION. Tax season surges (Apr/Jun/Sep/Dec)
    create temporary liquidity squeezes. Debt ceiling debates draw TGA to ~$0
    injecting massive liquidity. One of the most overlooked macro indicators.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/WTREGEN

A3  RRPONTSYD          Overnight Reverse Repo - ON RRP (Daily, D, 2003+)
    Money market funds parking cash at the Fed earning interest instead of
    lending it out. Rising RRP = money LEAVING the financial system = liquidity
    drain. Peaked $2.5T in mid-2022. Drained to near $0 by early 2024 as MMFs
    rotated into T-bills. RRP declining = cash re-entering markets.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/RRPONTSYD

    ** NET LIQUIDITY = WALCL - WTREGEN - RRPONTSYD **
    This is the single most important macro metric for risk assets according to
    many CT (crypto/trading) macro analysts. Rising = bullish, falling =
    bearish. SPX and BTC have historically tracked Net Liquidity closely.

A4  WSHOTSA            Treasury Securities Held Outright (Week Avg, W, 2002+)
    The Fed's Treasury holdings in its SOMA portfolio. Increasing during QE
    (buying bonds), decreasing during QT (letting bonds mature without
    reinvesting). Tracks the speed of QE/QT in Treasuries specifically.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/WSHOTSA

A5  WSHOMCB            MBS Held Outright (Wednesday Level, W, 2002+)
    Fed's mortgage-backed securities holdings. Same QE/QT tracking as WSHOTSA
    but for the mortgage market. MBS QT has been controversial because housing
    market sensitivity.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/WSHOMCB

A6  WRESBAL            Reserve Balances with Federal Reserve (Week Avg, W, 1984+)
    Total reserves held by banks at the Fed. As QT progresses, reserves shrink.
    Below ~$3T = potential "reserve scarcity" that caused the Sep 2019 repo
    market spike. Critical for predicting the next liquidity crisis.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/WRESBAL

A7  WORAL              Repurchase Agreements (Week Avg, W, 2002+)
    Fed's repo lending to banks. Spikes when banks need emergency cash (Sep
    2019 repo crisis, March 2020 COVID). Rising = stress in funding markets.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/WORAL

A8  WDTGAL             Treasury General Account (Wednesday Level, W, 2002+)
    Same as WTREGEN but Wednesday snapshot instead of weekly average. More
    useful for week-over-week change analysis.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/WDTGAL

A2. MONEY ON THE SIDELINES (7 series)

A9  RMFSL              Retail Money Market Funds (SA, Monthly, M, 1974+)
    THE "cash on the sidelines" metric. Retail MMF balances. Hit record ~$2.4T
    in 2024 as investors earned 5%+ risk-free. When RMFSL starts declining
    while equity markets are rising = money rotating FROM safety INTO risk
    assets (bullish). When RMFSL surging during equity correction = money
    fleeing to safety but creating "dry powder" for the next rally.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/RMFSL

A10 WRMFNS             Retail Money Market Funds (NSA, Weekly, W, 1974+)
    Same as RMFSL but weekly frequency and not seasonally adjusted. Faster
    signal for real-time money flow tracking.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/WRMFNS

A11 MMMFFAQ027S        Money Market Mutual Fund Total Financial Assets (Q, 1985+)
    Quarterly total MMF assets covering both retail AND institutional money
    market funds. Broader coverage than RMFSL alone. Includes prime, government,
    and tax-exempt funds.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/MMMFFAQ027S

A12 MZMNS              Money Zero Maturity (NSA, Monthly, M, 1959+)
    M2 minus small time deposits plus institutional MMFs. The broadest measure
    of immediately deployable liquid money. "Ultimate sidelines" metric because
    it captures ALL money that can be deployed instantly without penalty.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/MZMNS

A13 SAVINGSL           Savings Deposits at Commercial Banks (SA, Monthly, M, 1959+)
    Bank savings account balances including money market deposit accounts
    (MMDAs). Surged during COVID stimulus. When savings draw down = money being
    deployed into markets or spent in the real economy.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SAVINGSL

A14 TCDSL              Total Checkable Deposits (SA, Monthly, M, 1959+)
    Checking account balances. The most immediate deployable cash. Rising =
    more "at the ready" capital. Declining = money being deployed.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/TCDSL

A15 DSPIC96            Real Disposable Personal Income (SA, Monthly, M, 1959+)
    Consumer purchasing power after taxes and inflation. Rising real income =
    more potential investment capital flowing into 401(k)s, brokerages, etc.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DSPIC96

A3. INTERNATIONAL MONEY FLOWS — TIC DATA (5 series)

A16 FORLTTOTALNET69995 Foreign Net Purchases: All US Long-Term Securities (M, 1978+)
    Monthly aggregate of foreign buying minus selling of all US long-term
    securities (Treasuries, agencies, corporates, equities). Negative values =
    foreign investors are net sellers of US assets = bearish signal.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/FORLTTOTALNET69995

A17 FORLTEQTYNET99996  Foreign Net Purchases: US Equities (M, 1978+)
    THE international equity flow indicator. Monthly foreign net buying of US
    stocks. Track alongside USLTEQTYNET69995 (US buying of foreign stocks) for
    a complete picture of cross-border equity rotation. Consistently negative =
    capital flight from US equities.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/FORLTEQTYNET99996

A18 FORLTCORPNET69995  Foreign Net Purchases: US Corporate Bonds (M, 1978+)
    Foreign appetite for US corporate credit. Declining = diminishing foreign
    demand for US corporate debt, which can pressure credit spreads wider.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/FORLTCORPNET69995

A19 FORLTTREANET69995  Foreign Net Purchases: US Treasury Bonds & Notes (M, 1978+)
    Critical for understanding Treasury market demand. Japan and China are the
    largest foreign holders. Sustained net selling by foreign official entities
    = upward pressure on US yields, independent of Fed policy.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/FORLTTREANET69995

A20 USLTEQTYNET69995   US Net Purchases: Foreign Equities (M, 1978+)
    US money flowing OUT of domestic markets INTO foreign stocks. When this
    rises while FORLTEQTYNET99996 is negative = domestic AND foreign investors
    both avoiding US equities = strong bearish signal.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/USLTEQTYNET69995

A4. CREDIT / LENDING (5 series)

A21 TOTCI              Commercial & Industrial Loans, All Banks (SA, M, 1947+)
    Bank lending to businesses. Core measure of credit creation. Declining C&I
    loans = businesses not investing/expanding = recession signal. One of the
    most reliable coincident-to-slightly-lagging indicators.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/TOTCI

A22 TOTALSL            Total Consumer Credit Outstanding (SA, M, 1943+)
    Total consumer borrowing: credit cards, auto loans, student loans. Surging
    consumer credit = spending fueled by debt (unsustainable) or confidence
    (bullish). Context matters.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/TOTALSL

A23 CCLBS              Consumer Loans: Credit Cards & Revolving Plans (SA, M, 1968+)
    Revolving consumer credit (primarily credit card balances). Rapid increase
    while saving rate (PSAVERT) is declining = consumer stress / inflation
    pressure = future spending collapse risk.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CCLBS

A24 BUSLOANS           Commercial & Industrial Loans, All Banks (NSA, M, 1947+)
    Non-seasonally-adjusted version of TOTCI. Useful for raw trend analysis.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/BUSLOANS

A25 TOTBKCR            Bank Credit: Total Loans & Leases + Securities (SA, M, 1973+)
    Total credit extended by the US banking system. Broadest bank lending
    measure. Includes C&I loans, real estate loans, consumer loans, and
    securities holdings. Peaking bank credit = credit cycle top.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/TOTBKCR

-------------------------------
CATEGORY B: EMPLOYMENT / LABOR MARKET (~15 series)
-------------------------------

B1  PAYEMS             Total Nonfarm Payrolls (SA, Monthly, M, 1939+)
    The single most important monthly economic release. Released first Friday
    of every month. Typically the biggest market-moving data point. Normal
    growth: +150K to +250K/month. Below +100K = slowdown. Negative = likely
    recession. COVID: -20.5M in April 2020 (single worst month ever).
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/PAYEMS

B2  UNRATE             Unemployment Rate (SA, Monthly, M, 1948+)
    Most politically visible labor market indicator. Range: 2.5% (1953) to
    14.8% (Apr 2020). Pre-COVID record: 10.8% (1982). Typically changes
    ±0.1-0.3pp per month. Rises FAST in crisis, falls SLOW in recovery.
    Sahm Rule: 3-month avg UNRATE rising 0.5pp above prior 12-month low has
    ALWAYS coincided with recession (zero false positives since 1970).
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/UNRATE

B3  ICSA               Initial Jobless Claims (NSA, Weekly, W, 1967+)
    THE earliest labor market warning signal. Available WEEKLY. Part of the
    Conference Board LEI (inverted). Normal range: 200K-400K. Key signal:
    sustained 23%+ rise from cycle trough = recession historically follows in
    ~15 months. 400K+ sustained = recession-level stress. COVID peak: 6.1M.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/ICSA

B4  JTSJOL             JOLTS Job Openings: Total Nonfarm (SA, Monthly, M, 2000+)
    Measures labor demand. Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey. Declining
    openings while unemployment stable = softening labor market before it shows
    in UNRATE (leading). Peaked 12M+ in 2022.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/JTSJOL

B5  CES0500000003      Avg Hourly Earnings: Total Private (SA, Monthly, M, 2006+)
    Wage growth. Above 4-5% YoY = wage-price spiral risk (Fed hawkish). Below
    3% = wage growth consistent with 2% inflation target. Key Fed input.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CES0500000003

B6  LNS11300000        Labor Force Participation Rate (SA, Monthly, M, 1948+)
    Percentage of working-age population in the labor force. Structural
    decline since 2000 (aging demographics). Cyclical component: rising in
    strong labor markets (people re-entering workforce), falling when people
    give up looking. Pre-COVID peak: 67.3% (2000). COVID low: 60.1%.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/LNS11300000

B7  AWHMAN             Avg Weekly Hours: Manufacturing (SA, Monthly, M, 2006+)
    Conference Board LEI component. Employers cut hours BEFORE cutting jobs.
    Declining weekly hours = leading indicator of labor market weakening.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/AWHMAN

B8  U6RATE             U-6 Broad Unemployment Rate (SA, Monthly, M, 1994+)
    Includes discouraged workers and part-time workers who want full-time.
    Typically 1.5-2x higher than U-3 (UNRATE). Better measure of labor market
    slack. High U6 while U3 is low = hidden slack.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/U6RATE

B9  UEMPMEAN           Avg Duration of Unemployment, Weeks (SA, Monthly, M, 1948+)
    How long the average unemployed person has been looking. Rising = labor
    market deterioration (harder to find jobs). LAGGING indicator but confirms
    recession severity. Peaked 40+ weeks in 2010.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/UEMPMEAN

B10 EMRATIO            Employment-Population Ratio (SA, Monthly, M, 1948+)
    Percentage of total population that is employed. Better than UNRATE for
    structural analysis because it's not affected by labor force participation
    changes. Pre-COVID peak: 61.2% (2023). COVID low: 51.3%.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/EMRATIO

B11 CE16OV             Civilian Labor Force Level (SA, Monthly, M, 1948+)
    Total number of people working or looking for work. Growing = positive.
    Shrinking when participation rate is also falling = structural concern.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CE16OV

B12 USGOOD             All Employees: Goods-Producing (SA, Monthly, M, 1939+)
    Manufacturing, construction, mining employment. Cyclically sensitive.
    Declines early in slowdowns (LEADING relative to services employment).
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/USGOOD

B13 USPRIV             All Employees: Private Service-Providing (SA, M, 1939+)
    Services employment (77% of US jobs). Less cyclical than goods-producing
    but declining services hiring = broad-based slowdown.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/USPRIV

B14 JTSQUR             JOLTS Quits Rate (SA, Monthly, M, 2000+)
    Percentage of workers voluntarily quitting. High quits = workers confident
    they can find better jobs (bullish labor market). Declining quits = workers
    staying put = caution. Often called the "take this job and shove it"
    indicator. LEADING: quits turn before payrolls.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/JTSQUR

B15 CCSA               Continued Claims / Insured Unemployment (NSA, Weekly, W, 1967+)
    Number of people continuing to claim unemployment benefits (vs initial
    claims which are first-time filers). Lags ICSA by 1-2 weeks. Rising = people
    staying unemployed longer.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CCSA

CATEGORY C: INFLATION / PRICES (~12 series)
-------------------------------

C1  CPIAUCSL           CPI All Urban Consumers (SA, Monthly, M, 1947+)
    Most widely quoted inflation measure. Baskets of goods and services.
    Academic research (Cernohorsky 2016): CPI leads S&P 500 by ~21 months
    with inverse correlation (high CPI today = low S&P in 21 months).
    Range: -3% YoY (deflation) to 14.8% (1980). Fed unofficially watches it
    but officially targets PCE.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CPIAUCSL

C2  CPILFESL           Core CPI ex Food & Energy (SA, Monthly, M, 1957+)
    CPI minus volatile food and energy components. Better trend measure.
    Less "headline" impact but better signal for underlying inflation.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CPILFESL

C3  PCEPI              PCE Price Index (SA, Monthly, M, 1959+)
    Personal Consumption Expenditures price index. The Fed's OFFICIAL 2%
    inflation target measure. Differs from CPI: broader coverage, chain-
    weighted (accounts for substitution), healthcare weight difference.
    Typically runs 0.3-0.5% below CPI.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/PCEPI

C4  PCEPILFE           Core PCE ex Food & Energy (SA, Monthly, M, 1959+)
    THE number the Fed watches most closely. Core PCE YoY. Target: 2.0%.
    Above 3% = hawkish pressure. Below 1.5% = dovish pressure / deflation
    concern. Fed's Summary of Economic Projections (SEP) forecasts this.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/PCEPILFE

C5  PPIACO             PPI All Commodities (NSA, Monthly, M, 1913+)
    Producer Price Index. Pipeline inflation pressure. PPI leads CPI by 1-3
    months (businesses pass costs to consumers with a lag). Leading indicator
    for future CPI direction.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/PPIACO

C6  T5YIE              5-Year Breakeven Inflation Rate (Daily, D, 2003+)
    Market-implied inflation expectation: difference between 5Y nominal
    Treasury yield and 5Y TIPS yield. Forward-looking (LEADING). What the
    bond market expects inflation to average over the next 5 years.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/T5YIE

C7  T10YIE             10-Year Breakeven Inflation Rate (Daily, D, 2003+)
    Market-implied 10-year inflation expectation. Anchor for long-term
    inflation outlook. Below 2% = market expects Fed to miss target.
    Above 2.5% = market expects persistent above-target inflation.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/T10YIE

C8  MICH               Michigan 1-Year Inflation Expectations (Monthly, M, 1978+)
    Consumer survey of expected inflation over next 12 months. Anchored
    vs unanchored expectations matter. Surging = Fed credibility risk.
    Range: ~2-6%. Above 5% = de-anchoring concern.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/MICH

C9  PPIFGS             PPI Finished Goods (SA, Monthly, M, 1947+)
    PPI for finished consumer goods (excludes intermediate goods). More
    directly relevant to consumer prices than headline PPI.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/PPIFGS

C10 CPIHOSSL           CPI: Housing / Shelter (SA, Monthly, M, 1967+)
    Shelter component of CPI (~33% of index). CRITICAL: shelter lags real-time
    rents by 12-18 months due to BLS methodology (6-month rotation of rental
    sample). Known as "the lag" in Fed thinking. High shelter CPI while market
    rents are falling = misleading inflation signal.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CPIHOSSL

C11 MEDCPIM158SFRBCLE  Median CPI (SA, Monthly, M, 1983+)
    Cleveland Fed's median CPI: each month, sort all CPI component price
    changes and take the median. Less volatile than core CPI. Better for
    identifying persistent inflation trends.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/MEDCPIM158SFRBCLE

C12 PCE                Personal Consumption Expenditures (SA, Monthly, M, 1959+)
    Total dollar amount of consumer spending. Not an inflation rate — this is
    the LEVEL. Rising PCE = consumers spending more (nominal). Useful alongside
    PCEPI to decompose real vs nominal consumption.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/PCE

CATEGORY D: PMI / BUSINESS ACTIVITY (~12 series)
-------------------------------

D1  NAPM               ISM Manufacturing PMI Composite (NSA, Monthly, M, 1948+)
    THE single most widely followed business cycle indicator. Composite of 5
    sub-indices: New Orders, Production, Employment, Supplier Deliveries,
    Inventories. Above 50 = manufacturing expanding, below 50 = contracting.
    KEY THRESHOLDS:
      > 55: strong expansion (bullish)
      50-55: moderate expansion
      48-50: slight contraction (warning)
      42-48: contraction (bearish caution)
      < 42: BROAD ECONOMY RECESSION RISK (historical accuracy)
    Range: 29.4 (2008) to 77.5. Long-term avg: ~53.0.
    Can swing ±5-10 points at cyclical inflection points.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/NAPM

D2  NAPMNOI            ISM Mfg: New Orders Index (NSA, Monthly, M, 1948+)
    The MOST predictive ISM sub-index. Conference Board LEI component.
    12 out of 13 times since 1948: New Orders below 43.5 → recession followed.
    This is arguably more reliable than the headline PMI. New orders lead
    production by 1-3 months.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/NAPMNOI

D3  NAPMEI             ISM Mfg: Employment Index (NSA, Monthly, M, 1948+)
    Manufacturing hiring intentions. Below 50 = manufacturers plan to reduce
    headcount. Leads PAYEMS manufacturing component by 1-2 months.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/NAPMEI

D4  NAPMPRI            ISM Mfg: Prices Paid Index (NSA, Monthly, M, 1948+)
    What manufacturers report paying for inputs. Pipeline inflation gauge.
    Above 60 = increasing price pressure. Above 80 = severe inflation.
    Leads PPI and CPI by 1-3 months. During 2021-22: sustained 80+ readings.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/NAPMPRI

D5  INDPRO             Industrial Production Index (SA, Monthly, M, 1919+)
    Output of factories, mines, and utilities. Coincident indicator. Broad
    production measure. Declining 6+ months = recession likely. One of the
    four coincident indicators used by NBER to date recessions.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/INDPRO

D6  TCU                Capacity Utilization: Total Industry (SA, Monthly, M, 1967+)
    Percentage of industrial capacity in use. Above 85% = inflationary pressure
    (bottlenecks, need for capital investment). Below 75% = slack in the
    economy (disinflationary). COVID low: 64.2%.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/TCU

D7  DGORDER            Durable Goods Orders (SA, Monthly, M, 1992+)
    New orders for long-lasting manufactured goods. Conference Board LEI
    component. Volatile month-to-month but core orders (ex-transportation)
    is a cleaner signal. LEADS industrial production by 3-6 months.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGORDER

D8  BUSINV             Total Business Inventories (SA, Monthly, M, 1992+)
    Inventory levels across manufacturing, wholesale, and retail. Build-up
    above sales trend = inventory overhang = future production cuts. Lean
    inventories = need to restock = future production increase.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/BUSINV

D9  RSAFS              Advance Retail Sales (SA, Monthly, M, 1992+)
    Consumer spending at retail stores (ex-food services). ~30% of total PCE.
    Real-time consumer health check. Declining 2+ consecutive months = consumer
    pulling back.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/RSAFS

D10 AMDMNO             Mfg New Orders: Durable Goods (SA, Monthly, M, 1992+)
    Same as DGORDER but adjusted differently. Alternative series for
    cross-validation.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/AMDMNO

D11 AMTMNO             Mfg New Orders: Total Manufacturing (SA, Monthly, M, 1992+)
    Total manufacturing new orders (durable + nondurable). Broader than
    durable goods alone. Leading indicator for industrial production.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/AMTMNO

D12 ISRATIO            Business Inventories to Sales Ratio (SA, Monthly, M, 1992+)
    How many months of sales are sitting in inventory. Rising ratio = goods
    piling up unsold = future production cuts. Falling = demand outpacing
    supply = need to increase production. Cyclical range: ~1.25-1.45.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/ISRATIO

-------------------------------
CATEGORY E: FINANCIAL CONDITIONS / STRESS (~7 series)
-------------------------------

E1  NFCI               Chicago Fed National Financial Conditions Index (W, 1971+)
    105 financial indicators combined. Above 0 = financial conditions tighter
    than average. Below 0 = looser than average. Weekly frequency. Spikes
    above +1 = financial crisis territory (2008: +3.5, COVID: +1.5).
    LEADING for equity markets: tightening conditions precede selloffs.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/NFCI

E2  NFCIRISK           NFCI Risk Sub-Index (Weekly, W, 1971+)
    Risk component of NFCI. Captures volatility and funding risk specifically.
    More sensitive to acute stress events than overall NFCI.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/NFCIRISK

E3  NFCILEVERAGE       NFCI Leverage Sub-Index (Weekly, W, 1971+)
    Leverage component of NFCI. Captures debt and margin conditions. Rising =
    increasing leverage in the financial system. Peak leverage often coincides
    with market tops.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/NFCILEVERAGE

E4  STLFSI4            St. Louis Fed Financial Stress Index (Weekly, W, 1993+)
    18 weekly financial stress indicators. Above 0 = above-average stress.
    More intuitive than NFCI for many practitioners. COVID peak: +5.8 (highest
    ever, exceeding 2008's +5.3). Below -0.5 = very low stress (bullish).
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/STLFSI4

E5  VIXCLS             CBOE Volatility Index: VIX (Daily, D, 1990+)
    [ALREADY IN EXISTING CODE AS VIX.csv]
    S&P 500 30-day implied volatility. "Fear gauge." Normal: 12-20. Elevated:
    20-30. Fear: 30-40. Panic: 40+. Record: 82.69 (Mar 16, 2020).

E6  VXVCLS             CBOE 3-Month VIX (VXV) (Daily, D, 2007+)
    93-day implied volatility vs VIX's 30-day. VIX/VXV ratio indicates term
    structure: VIX > VXV = backwardation (near-term fear exceeds long-term
    fear) = often near market bottoms.
    URL: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/VXVCLS

E7  USEPUINDXD         Economic Policy Uncertainty Index (Daily, D, 1985+)
    News-based index counting articles with "economic," "policy," "uncertainty"
    in major US newspapers. Spike to 2500+ = extreme uncertainty (trade war,

3. MONEY FLOW FRAMEWORK DEEP DIVE
================================================================================

The single most important framework for predicting medium-term equity market
direction is the Net Liquidity / Money Flow framework:

PRIMARY FRAMEWORK: Net Liquidity = WALCL - TGA - RRP

  WALCL (Fed Balance Sheet): The "source" of liquidity.
    - QE (WALCL rising) = Fed creates reserves to buy bonds = banks have more
      reserves = more capacity to lend and invest = BULLISH for risk assets
    - QT (WALCL falling) = Fed shrinks balance sheet = reserves drained =
      BEARISH headwind

  TGA (Treasury General Account): The "drain" #1.
    - When Treasury collects taxes or issues debt: TGA RISES → money moves
      FROM bank reserves TO Treasury's Fed account → less money in system
    - When Treasury spends: TGA FALLS → money flows to households/businesses →
      more money in system
    - Tax seasons (Apr, Jun, Sep, Dec 15): TGA surges $100-300B → temporary
      liquidity drain → often coincides with short-term market weakness
    - Debt ceiling: TGA drained to near $0 → massive liquidity injection →
      often coincides with strong market rallies

  RRP (Overnight Reverse Repo): The "drain" #2.
    - Money Market Funds park cash at Fed instead of lending in repo market
      or buying T-bills
    - RRP peaked $2.5T in June 2022 (everyone chasing 5%+ risk-free at Fed)
    - RRP drained to near $0 by early 2024 as MMFs rotated into T-bills
      (T-bill yields exceeded RRP rate)
    - RRP declining = money LEAVING Fed and ENTERING financial markets =
      BULLISH for risk assets
    - RRP rising = money LEAVING markets and parking at Fed = BEARISH

THE SEQUENCE (How money flows into/out of markets):
  1. Fed Net Liquidity (WALCL - TGA - RRP) starts rising
  2. Money market fund balances (RMFSL) peak and start declining
  3. Equity fund flows turn positive (ICI weekly data)
  4. Margin debt starts expanding (FINRA data)
  5. S&P 500 rises

REVERSAL SEQUENCE:
  1. Fed Net Liquidity starts falling (QT + TGA surge + RRP surge)
  2. Money market fund balances start rising again (RMFSL increasing)
  3. Equity fund flows turn negative
  4. Margin debt contracts
  5. S&P 500 declines

THE CASH ROTATION CYCLE:
  "Sidelines" cash (RMFSL + SAVINGSL + MZMNS) at all-time highs
    → Fear subsides → cash earns less than risk assets
    → Cash rotates into equities → SPX rises
    → Eventually all cash deployed → no more "fuel"
    → Market tops → Correction → Cash flees back to MMFs (RMFSL surges)
    → Cycle repeats

Key money flow signals:
  - RMFSL at ATH while SPX at ATH = late cycle (cash fully deployed)
  - RMFSL at ATH while SPX correcting = mid-correction (dry powder building)
  - RMFSL declining while SPX rising = early-to-mid cycle (rotation in)
  - TIC data negative (foreign selling US equities) while SPX going up =
    domestic buyers absorbing = fragile rally

5. CHANGE VELOCITY ANALYSIS
================================================================================

How fast do indicators typically move? This is CRITICAL for token design:
"rising fast" vs "rising slowly" must be calibrated to each indicator's

6. INDICATOR RELATIONSHIPS & SEQUENCING
================================================================================

Money Flow → Market Direction (PRIMARY FRAMEWORK):
  Fed Net Liquidity (WALCL - TGA - RRP) rising
    → Money leaves MMFs (RMFSL declining)
    → Equity fund flows turn positive (ICI data)
    → Margin debt expands (FINRA data)
    → SPX rises

Recession Sequence (12-24 month lead time):
  T10Y2Y inverts (negative ≥ 30 days)
    → (12-24 months lag)
  Credit spreads widen (BAMLH0A0HYM2 > 600bp)
    → NFCI turns positive
    → (0-3 months)
  NAPM drops below 50 → NAPMNOI below 43.5
    → (1-3 months)
  Durable Goods Orders (DGORDER) decline
    → Average weekly hours (AWHMAN) cut
    → (1-3 months)
  PAYEMS growth slows to < 100K/month
    → ICSA rises 23%+ from cycle trough
    → (3-9 months)
  UNRATE rises (Sahm Rule triggered)
    → NBER declares recession
    → FEDFUNDS cut aggressively
    → WALCL expands (QE)

Credit Stress → Equity Decline:
  BAMLH0A0HYM2 widening > 600bp → (1-2 months) → SPX drawdown begins
  NFCI > 0 → confirming credit stress
  BAA10Y widening → confirming broader credit deterioration

Dollar → Everything Chain:
  DTWEXBGS strengthening → Global financial conditions tighten
    → EM currencies weaken (DEXCHUS↑, DEXKOUS↑, DEXMXUS↑)
    → Commodities fall (DCOILWTICO↓, PCOPPUSDM↓)
    → US multinational earnings headwind
    → TIC flows: foreign selling of US (FORLTEQTYNET99996 negative)
    → EM equities underperform US

Carry Trade Dynamics:
  IRSTCB01JPM156N << FEDFUNDS (spread > 300bp) = large carry incentive
    → DEXJPUS steadily rising (JPY weakening, risk-on)
    → Leveraged carry positions accumulate (borrow JPY, buy USD assets)
    → TRIGGER: BOJ hike or US recession scare
    → DEXJPUS crashes (JPY strengthens 5-10% in days)
    → Forced carry unwind → Nikkei -5% to -12% single day
    → Spills to US futures → VIX spike → EM FX selloff

Inflation → Fed → Economy Chain:
  CPIAUCSL (or Core PCE) rises above target
    → (3-6 months) FEDFUNDS hikes begin
    → (12-24 months LONG AND VARIABLE LAG)
    → Economy slows → NAPM drops
    → Labor market weakens → CPI moderates
    → FEDFUNDS cuts begin

Consumer → Spending → Economy Chain:
  UMCSENT drops below 60
    → (1-3 months) Retail sales growth slows
    → (3-6 months) GDP growth decelerates
    → Personal saving rate (PSAVERT) rises (precautionary saving)
    → Investment spending contracts
    → Recession

8. NON-FRED DATA GAP ANALYSIS
================================================================================

Critical indicators NOT available via FRED API that require alternative sources:

1. FINRA MARGIN DEBT (Monthly)
   THE leverage indicator. Margin debt peaks near market tops and troughs near
   market bottoms. Oct 2021 peak: $936B. Jun 2022 trough: $600B.
   Source: https://www.finra.org/investors/margin-statistics
   Download format: CSV/XLS directly from FINRA website.

2. ICI MUTUAL FUND / ETF WEEKLY FLOWS
   THE money flow signal. Weekly estimated flows into equity vs bond mutual
   funds and ETFs. Shows exactly where retail/institutional money is rotating.
   Source: https://www.ici.org/research/stats/combined_flows
   Format: Excel/PDF. Auto-download may require parsing.

3. ISM SERVICES PMI (NMI)
   Services sector PMI — 77% of US economy. Manufacturing PMI (NAPM) is
   available on FRED, but Services PMI requires direct ISM subscription.
   Source: https://www.ismworld.org/supply-management-news-and-reports/reports/ism-report-on-business/services/
   Cost: ISM charges for data access.

4. CONFERENCE BOARD CONSUMER CONFIDENCE INDEX
   Alternative consumer sentiment measure to Michigan (UMCSENT which IS on
   FRED). Different methodology, sometimes diverges significantly.
   Source: https://www.conference-board.org/topics/consumer-confidence

5. CFTC COMMITMENT OF TRADERS (COT)
   Weekly futures positioning data. Shows speculative vs commercial positioning
   in equity index futures, Treasury futures, FX, commodities.
   Source: https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/index.htm
   Extremely valuable for contrarian signals (extreme speculative positioning).

6. ICE DOLLAR INDEX (DXY)
   The most widely quoted dollar index in financial media. NOT on FRED.
   FRED alternative: DTWEXBGS (broader, trade-weighted, better for macro).
   Source: Bloomberg, Reuters, ICE (proprietary).
