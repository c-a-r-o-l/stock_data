A3. SPY-RELATIVE PRICES — Stock prices normalized by SPX

     Market Logic: Removes broad-market drift so the Transformer sees

     Data Type   : FLOAT

     Norm Strategy: Z-score. Values are already relative, so cross-sectional
                   comparison is valid.

[11] SPY_H
     Meaning     : Stock HIGH / SPX HIGH, scaled by 1e6.
     Market Logic: Intra-bar relative strength ceiling.
     Data Type   : FLOAT
     Norm Strategy: Z-score.

[12] SPY_L
     Meaning     : Stock LOW / SPX LOW, scaled by 1e6.
     Market Logic: Intra-bar relative weakness floor.
     Data Type   : FLOAT
     Norm Strategy: Z-score.

[13] SPY_C
     Meaning     : Stock CLOSE / SPX CLOSE, scaled by 1e6.
     Market Logic: Relative closing strength. The primary relative-strength

     Data Type   : FLOAT
     Norm Strategy: Z-score.

A4. HEIKIN-ASHI PRICES — Smoothed candlestick values
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

     Heikin-Ashi (HA) candles filter noise by averaging OHLC:
       HA_C = (O + H + L + C) / 4
       HA_O = (HA_O[t-1] + HA_C[t-1]) / 2
       HA_H = MAX(H, HA_O, HA_C)
       HA_L = MIN(L, HA_O, HA_C)
     First bar: HA_O = OPEN, HA_C = (O+H+L+C)/4

     Market Logic: HA candles produce smoother trends with fewer false
                   reversals. A sequence of HA candles with no lower wicks
                   (HA_O = HA_L) signals a strong uptrend. HA is a form of
                   low-pass filter on price — the Transformer can attend to
                   raw OHLC for high-frequency detail and HA for trend.

     Both the stock ticker and SPY-relative prices have HA counterparts.

--- Stock Heikin-Ashi ---

[14] HA_O    — Heikin-Ashi open for the stock.               FLOAT, Z-score
[15] HA_H    — Heikin-Ashi high for the stock.               FLOAT, Z-score
[16] HA_L    — Heikin-Ashi low for the stock.                FLOAT, Z-score
[17] HA_C    — Heikin-Ashi close for the stock.              FLOAT, Z-score

--- SPY Heikin-Ashi ---

[18] HA_O_SPY  — Heikin-Ashi open for SPY-relative.          FLOAT, Z-score
[19] HA_H_SPY  — Heikin-Ashi high for SPY-relative.          FLOAT, Z-score
[20] HA_L_SPY  — Heikin-Ashi low for SPY-relative.           FLOAT, Z-score
[21] HA_C_SPY  — Heikin-Ashi close for SPY-relative.         FLOAT, Z-score

A5. SWING HIGH / LOW DETECTION — DayH, DayL, DayCH, DayCL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

     These use the BB_DAY_H and BB_DAY_L functions from Functions.txt.
     BB_DAY_H(Price, Length): counts how many consecutive bars have a HIGH
     higher than all previous HIGH for Length bars. When the sequence breaks,
     the counter resets. Essentially: "how many bars since a new N-bar high?"

     BB_DAY_L: same logic but for LOWs (counting lower lows).

     Market Logic: Encodes market structure — higher highs (uptrend) and
                   lower lows (downtrend). A DayH value of 20 (at the limit)
                   means the current bar broke to a new N-bar high (breakout).
                   These are structural features (Principle #4).

[22] DayH
     Meaning  : Count of consecutive new N-bar highs (lookback varies by
                timeframe: 200 daily, 50 weekly, 20 monthly).
                Value 20 = breakout (new high confirmed).
     Data Type: INTEGER (0–20+)
     Norm Strategy: Min-Max to [0, 1] dividing by lookback length.

[23] DayL
     Meaning  : Count of consecutive new N-bar lows.
                Value 20 = breakdown (new low confirmed).
     Data Type: INTEGER (0–20+)
     Norm Strategy: Min-Max to [0, 1].

[24] DayCH
     Meaning  : DayH applied to CLOSE instead of HIGH.
                More sensitive to trend direction changes.
     Data Type: INTEGER
     Norm Strategy: Min-Max to [0, 1].

[25] DayCL
     Meaning  : DayL applied to CLOSE instead of LOW.
     Data Type: INTEGER
     Norm Strategy: Min-Max to [0, 1].

--- Polarized Fractal Efficiency (PFE) ---

     PFE measures how efficiently price travels from point A to point B.
     Formula: PFE = sqrt((CP-CP[N])^2 + N^2) / sum of sqrt(1 + (CP[i]-CP[i-1])^2)
     Then smoothed with 5-bar EMA.

     Market Logic: PFE > 50 = strong trend (efficient movement).
                   PFE < 30 = choppy/consolidating market.
                   Distinct from MA crossover — measures path efficiency,
                   not just price level.

A7. TREND & REGRESSION — Fit, Trigger, ITrend
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

     These form a regression-based trend system using an alpha=0.07 smoothing
     filter applied to (H+L)/2 (the mid-price):

       ITrend  = alpha-adjusted EMA with smoothing (Ehlers-style filter)
       Trigger = 2 * ITrend[t] - ITrend[t-2]  (momentum projection)
       Fit     = 2nd-order low-pass filter with period=10 (smoother than ITrend)

     Market Logic: This is a dual-smoother trend system. ITrend is the fast
                   line, Fit is the slow line, Trigger is the signal line.
                   When ITrend crosses above Trigger, uptrend begins.
                   The separate Fit provides a slower, more reliable trend
                   baseline. Together they encode trend direction, strength,
                   and acceleration.

[51] Fit
     Meaning  : Low-pass filtered mid-price (period=10, critical damping).
                The "slow" trend line. Acts as dynamic support/resistance.
     Data Type: FLOAT (price scale)
     Norm Strategy: Divide by CLOSE (ratio to price), then Z-score.

[52] Trigger
     Meaning  : 2*ITrend - ITrend[t-2]. Forward-projected trend value.
                Acts as a faster signal line.
     Data Type: FLOAT (price scale)
     Norm Strategy: Divide by CLOSE, then Z-score.

[53] Itrend
     Meaning  : Instantaneous trend (Ehlers alpha filter on mid-price).
                The "fast" trend line.
     Data Type: FLOAT (price scale)
     Norm Strategy: Divide by CLOSE, then Z-score.

[54] Trigger_Itrend_OHLC
     Meaning  : Combined state of Trigger + ITrend relative to OHLC.
                Values: -2,-1,0,1,2. See Functions.txt lines 438-467.
                  +2 = Trigger < LOW  AND ITrend <= LOW   (strong bullish)
                  +1 = Trigger < CLOSE (partial bullish)
                   0 = neutral (consolidation/no clear signal)
                  -1 = Trigger > CLOSE (partial bearish)
                  -2 = Trigger > HIGH AND ITrend >= HIGH  (strong bearish)
     Market Logic: Encodes whether the fast trend system confirms or
                   contradicts the bar's OHLC. +2 means both ITrend and
                   Trigger are below the entire bar (price breaking up
                   through resistance). This is a structural feature
                   (Principle #4).
     Data Type: INTEGER (-2 to +2)
     Norm Strategy: Divide by 2 → range [-1, 1]. Identity is also fine
                   since the range is already compact and symmetric.

--- SPY counterparts ---

     Same indicators computed on SPY-relative prices (SPY_H, SPY_L, etc.)

[55] Fit_SPY              — Fit on SPY-relative mid-price.    FLOAT, Z-score
[56] Trigger_SPY          — Trigger on SPY-relative.          FLOAT, Z-score
[57] Itrend_SPY           — ITrend on SPY-relative.           FLOAT, Z-score
[58] Trigger_Itrend_OHLC_SPY — OHLC-relative state for SPY.   INTEGER [-2,2]

A8. VOLATILITY & BANDS — STD, Bollinger, ATR, BIAS, TTM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[59] STD_20
     Meaning  : 20-bar standard deviation of CLOSE.
     Market Logic: Rolling volatility. High STD_20 = turbulent market.
                   Low STD_20 = quiet/compressed market (preceding expansion).
     Data Type: FLOAT (price scale, always >= 0)
     Norm Strategy: Divide by CLOSE (relative volatility), then Z-score.

[60] BBH_20
     Meaning  : Bollinger Band High = SMA_020 + 2 * STD_20.
     Market Logic: Upper 2-sigma boundary. Price > BBH_20 = overbought
                   (2.5% probability under normal distribution). Sustained
                   rides above BBH signal strong momentum, not mean reversion.
     Data Type: FLOAT (price scale)
     Norm Strategy: (CLOSE - BBH_20) / STD_20 → Z-score of distance from band,
                   already normalized.

[61] BBL_20
     Meaning  : Bollinger Band Low = SMA_020 - 2 * STD_20.
     Market Logic: Lower 2-sigma boundary. Price < BBL_20 = oversold.
     Data Type: FLOAT (price scale)
     Norm Strategy: (CLOSE - BBL_20) / STD_20 → Z-score.

[62] ATR
     Meaning  : Average True Range (20-bar).
                TR = MAX(H-L, |H-C[t-1]|, |L-C[t-1]|)
     Market Logic: Volatility that accounts for gaps. ATR is used for
                   position sizing (1 ATR = normal daily/weekly range).
                   Rising ATR = expanding volatility regime.
     Data Type: FLOAT (price scale, >= 0)
     Norm Strategy: Divide by CLOSE → relative ATR, then Z-score.

[63] ATR_200  (Daily only)
     Meaning  : 200-bar ATR. Long-term volatility baseline.
     Market Logic: Current ATR vs. ATR_200 ratio shows whether current
                   volatility is elevated vs. historical norm.
     Data Type: FLOAT
     Norm Strategy: Divide by CLOSE, then Z-score.

[64] BIAS_Diff
     Meaning  : Composite bias indicator. Measures the gap between EMA_020
                and Fit, then compares it to two dynamic thresholds
                (BIAS_Diff_A = distance from historical high of bias;
                 BIAS_Diff_B = distance from 1-sigma below average bias).
                The function selects the minimum of these two when both are
                negative (oriented divergence) and handles mixed-sign cases
                with Heikin-Ashi and price-action confirmation.
     Market Logic: Detects when price has diverged too far from the smoothed
                   trend (EMA vs. regression). Negative values = price is
                   stretched below trend (potential mean-reversion long).
                   Positive = stretched above (potential mean-reversion short).
                   This is NOT a simple Bollinger %B — it uses two adaptive
                   thresholds and requires structural confirmation (HA, inside
                   bars, gap patterns) before signaling.
     Data Type: FLOAT (can be positive or negative)
     Norm Strategy: Z-score.

[65] TTM
     Meaning  : 2 * STD_20 - 1.5 * ATR.
     Market Logic: When STD_20 > 0.75 * ATR, TTM > 0 (trending — directional
                   volatility exceeds noise). When STD_20 < 0.75 * ATR, TTM < 0
                   (choppy — noise exceeds trend). A regime classifier: trending
                   vs. mean-reverting environment.
     Data Type: FLOAT (can be negative)
     Norm Strategy: Z-score.

A9. PRICE-VOLUME TREND — PV_BULL / PV_BEAR state machine
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

     Reference: Functions.txt lines 102-170 (BB_Trend_S_PV).

     This is a state machine that tracks bullish and bearish price-volume
     phases. It's the most structurally complex feature group.

     Core Logic:
     - A bull phase starts when CLOSE crosses above the bear stop level
       (PV_BEAR_STOP) and confirms with volume > average.
     - A bear phase starts when CLOSE crosses below the bull stop level
       (PV_BULL_STOP) and confirms with volume > average.
     - Each phase has a sequential counter (STEP) that increments when
       the stop level changes.
     - Trend_PV = +PV_BULL when bull active, -PV_BEAR when bear active.

     Market Logic: Encodes market microstructure (Principle #4).
                   These phases capture the ebb and flow of buying/selling
                   pressure with volume confirmation. PV_BULL_CNT tells you
                   how long the current bull run has lasted — duration is a
                   key feature for regime persistence.

[66] Trend_PV
     Meaning  : Current PV state (+N = bull phase N, -N = bear phase N, 0 = neutral).
     Data Type: INTEGER
     Data Range: Typically -10 to +10
     Norm Strategy: Tanh normalization: tanh(x / 5) → range (-1, 1).
                   Or Z-score if distribution permits.

--- Bull Phase Group ---

[67] PV_BULL
     Meaning  : Bull phase active flag. 1 = in bull phase, 0 = not.
     Data Type: INTEGER (0 or 1)
     Norm Strategy: Identity (already binary).

[68] PV_BULL_STEP
     Meaning  : Sequential bull phase counter (increments each new bull phase).
     Data Type: INTEGER (1, 2, 3, ...)
     Norm Strategy: Log(1 + x) then Z-score, or clip at 10.

[69] PV_BULL_START
     Meaning  : LOW price at which the current bull phase started.
     Data Type: FLOAT (price)
     Norm Strategy: (CLOSE - PV_BULL_START) / CLOSE → % gain from start, Z-score.

[70] PV_BULL_STOP
     Meaning  : LOW price stop level for the current bull phase.
                If price drops below this, the bull phase ends.
     Data Type: FLOAT (price)
     Norm Strategy: (CLOSE - PV_BULL_STOP) / CLOSE → Z-score.

[71] PV_BULL_CNT
     Meaning  : Number of bars elapsed in the current bull phase.
     Data Type: INTEGER
     Norm Strategy: Log(1 + x), Z-score.

[72] PV_BULL_H
     Meaning  : Highest HIGH reached during the current bull phase.
     Data Type: FLOAT (price)
     Norm Strategy: (PV_BULL_H - CLOSE) / CLOSE → % drawdown from peak, Z-score.

--- Bear Phase Group ---

[73] PV_BEAR          — Bear active flag. 0/1.                Identity
[74] PV_BEAR_STEP     — Sequential bear phase counter.        Log+Z-score
[75] PV_BEAR_START    — HIGH price at bear phase start.       % from start, Z-score
[76] PV_BEAR_STOP     — HIGH price stop level for bear phase. % to stop, Z-score

A10. BEAR/BULL TREND — Stochastic-anchored trend detection
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

     Reference: Functions.txt lines 257-425 (BB_Trend_S_BEAR_BULL).

     This is a multi-condition trend classifier. It evaluates 15+ distinct
     conditions to determine whether the bar is bullish (Bull=2) or bearish
     (Bear=2), then tracks consecutive counts.

     Key conditions checked (Bull example):
     - "Bone": CP > max(ITrend, Trigger, Fit) AND CP > EMA_020 AND ITrend rising
     - "Head and Toe": Fit < Trigger < ITrend < EMA_020 with close above Trigger
     - All MAs aligned: EMA_020 > EMA_020[t-1] AND ITrend rising AND Fit rising
     - MACD breakout: MACD_C > MACD_H AND CP > max(ITrend, Trigger)
     - MACD + 3Bar: MACD_DIFF > 0 AND Trend_S_3Bar = 1
     - Simple: CP > EMA_020 AND CP > max(ITrend, Trigger)
     - Fallback: CP > OPEN
     - Demark reversal: prevents whipsaw (if 2 bull bars then 1 bear but
       Demark support holds, stay bull)

     Market Logic: This is a fusion classifier — it combines trend,
                   momentum, and structural signals into a single bull/bear
                   call. The 15 conditions are ordered by reliability.
                   The Stochastic sub-indicators add a second layer
                   (Stochastic_Bull = 1-5, increasing confidence).

[79] Trend_BEAR_BULL
     Meaning  : 1 = bull active, -1 = bear active, 0 = neutral.
     Data Type: INTEGER (-1, 0, 1)
     Data Range: -1, 0, 1
     Norm Strategy: Identity (already [-1, 0, 1]).

[80] Bull_CNT
     Meaning  : Number of consecutive bars with Bull > 0.
     Data Type: INTEGER (0+)
     Norm Strategy: Log(1 + x), Z-score.

[81] Stochastic_Bull
     Meaning  : Confidence level of bull signal (0–5, higher = stronger).
                Uses MACD and Stochastic oscillator to rank the bull case.
     Data Type: INTEGER (0–5)
     Norm Strategy: Divide by 5 → [0, 1].

[82] Stochastic_Bull_CNT
     Meaning  : Consecutive bars with Stochastic_Bull > 0.
     Data Type: INTEGER
     Norm Strategy: Log(1 + x), Z-score.

[83] Bear_CNT
     Meaning  : Number of consecutive bars with Bear > 0.
     Data Type: INTEGER (0+)
     Norm Strategy: Log(1 + x), Z-score.

[84] Stochastic_Bear
     Meaning  : Confidence level of bear signal (0–5).
     Data Type: INTEGER (0–5)
     Norm Strategy: Divide by 5 → [0, 1] (then optionally negate for direction).

[85] Stochastic_Bear_CNT
     Meaning  : Consecutive bars with Stochastic_Bear > 0.
     Data Type: INTEGER
     Norm Strategy: Log(1 + x), Z-score.

A11. PUSH/DIFF — Divergence between price and momentum
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

     Reference: Functions.txt lines 216-254 (BB_Trend_S_Push).

     Push_Diff measures divergence between a custom price transformation
     and its smoothed average:

       Push     = MACD( XAverage(4*CP - 2*CP[t-2] - (H+L), 2),  12, 26 )
       Push_Diff = 100 * (3*Push - XAverage(Push, 10)) / EMA_020

     Trend_S_Push encodes the trend of Push_Diff:
       +3 = positive, accelerating above zero
       +2 = positive, slowing but still above zero
       +1 = positive, decelerating toward zero
       -1 = negative, recovering toward zero
       -2 = negative, slowing but still below zero
       -3 = negative, accelerating below zero

     Market Logic: Push_Diff measures the rate of change of a smoothed
                   price derivative. When Push_Diff crosses above 0, price
                   momentum is accelerating faster than its average.
                   This is a leading indicator — it turns before MACD.
                   The Bull/Bear counters track persistence.

[93] Trend_S_Push_Diff    — Trend state of Push_Diff.    INTEGER [-3,3], /3 → [-1,1]
[94] Push_Diff            — Raw Push_Diff value.         FLOAT, Z-score
[95] Push_Diff_Bull_CNT   — Consecutive bullish Push bars.  INTEGER, Log+Z-score
[96] Push_Diff_Bear_CNT   — Consecutive bearish Push bars.  INTEGER, Log+Z-score

A12. COMPOSITE TREND SIGNALS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[101] Trend_S_3Bar
      Meaning  : 3-bar reversal pattern (Functions.txt lines 1-74).
                 A state machine tracking swing highs/lows:
                 When CLOSE > H_Limit[t-1]: start bull phase (ThreeBar=1).
                   H_B1/B2/B3 = last 3 confirmed highs.
                   H_Limit = max of H_B1,B2,B3 (resistance).
                 When CLOSE < L_Limit[t-1]: start bear phase (ThreeBar=-1).
                   L_Limit = min of L_B1,B2,B3 (support).
                 Resets daily at 08:34 (intraday) or when BarNumber < 3.
      Market Logic: Classic swing-point detection. A flip from -1 to +1
                    is a "3-bar reversal" — price broke above the last 3
                    swing highs. Used as a key confirmation in the
                    Bear/Bull classifier and Bottom/Mid/Top pattern logic.
      Data Type: INTEGER (-1, 0, 1)
      Norm Strategy: Identity (already compact and directional).

[102] Trend_S_HKA
      Meaning  : Heikin-Ashi trend for stock (Functions.txt lines 176-213).
                   +2 = HA_O < HA_C AND HA_O = HA_L (bull candle with no lower wick)
                   +1 = HA_O < HA_C (bull candle with lower wick)
                   -1 = HA_O > HA_C (bear candle with upper wick)
                   -2 = HA_O > HA_C AND HA_O = HA_H (bear candle with no upper wick)
      Market Logic: HA candle quality. +2 = strong bull (no lower shadow =
                    buyers dominated entire bar). -2 = strong bear. The
                    distinction between +1 and +2 is critical — it separates
                    "grinding" trends from "surging" trends.
      Data Type: INTEGER (-2, -1, 0, 1, 2)
      Norm Strategy: Divide by 2 → [-1, 1].

[103] Trend_S_HKA_SPY
      Meaning  : Same as Trend_S_HKA but on SPY-relative HA candles.
      Data Type: INTEGER (-2 to +2)
      Norm Strategy: /2 → [-1, 1].

[104] TTM_Momentum
      Meaning  : 100 * (2*(C+H+L) - 3*(Highest(H,20) + Lowest(L,20)))
                       / (Highest(H,20) + Lowest(L,20))
      Market Logic: Normalized position within the 20-bar range. If price
                    is near the 20-bar high, TTM_Momentum ≈ +100. Near the
                    20-bar low, TTM_Momentum ≈ -100. This is analogous to
                    the Stochastic oscillator but uses H+L instead of just C,
                    and applies a 3x weighting to the extremes. More sensitive
                    to range expansion than standard oscillators.
      Data Type: FLOAT (typically -100 to +100, but can exceed)
      Norm Strategy: Divide by 100 → [-1, 1] approximately. Clip outliers.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A13. MACD — Moving Average Convergence Divergence
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

     Formula:       MACD_C    = EMA(CLOSE, 12) - EMA(CLOSE, 26)
                    MACD_DIFF = 3 * (MACD_C - EMA(MACD_C, 10))
                    MACD_H    = EMA(MACD_C, 10) + STDDEV(MACD_C, 10)
                    MACD_L    = EMA(MACD_C, 10) - STDDEV(MACD_C, 10)

     Market Logic: The standard MACD system. MACD_DIFF is the histogram —
                   when it crosses above 0, MACD line crosses above signal
                   (bullish crossover). MACD_H and MACD_L form Bollinger-like
                   bands around the signal line to detect extreme momentum.

     NOTE: In the daily dataset, there is also a Trend_S_MACD_Diff column
           that is computed but its output is NOT exported to the CSV.
           The raw MACD components are exported instead.

[105] MACD_C
      Meaning  : MACD line (12/26 EMA difference).
                 Positive = short-term trend above long-term trend.
      Data Type: FLOAT (price scale)
      Norm Strategy: Divide by CLOSE → relative MACD, then Z-score.

[106] MACD_H
      Meaning  : Signal line + 1 STD (upper Bollinger Band on MACD).
                 When MACD_C > MACD_H, momentum is extremely bullish.
      Data Type: FLOAT
      Norm Strategy: (MACD_C - MACD_H) / STDDEV(MACD_C, 10) → Z-score.

[107] MACD_L
      Meaning  : Signal line - 1 STD (lower Bollinger Band on MACD).
      Data Type: FLOAT
      Norm Strategy: (MACD_C - MACD_L) / STDDEV(MACD_C, 10) → Z-score.

[108] MACD_DIFF
      Meaning  : MACD histogram = 3 * (MACD_C - Signal).
      Data Type: FLOAT
      Norm Strategy: Z-score.

A14. OSCILLATORS — Stochastic & RSI
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[109] oSlowK
      Meaning  : Stochastic Slow %K (10, 3, 10).
                 100 * (CLOSE - Lowest(LOW, 10)) / (Highest(HIGH, 10) - Lowest(LOW, 10))
                 then smoothed with 3-period SMA.
      Market Logic: Position of close within the 10-bar range. >80 = overbought,
                    <20 = oversold. The Slow variant reduces whipsaws vs. Fast %K.
      Data Type: FLOAT [0–100]
      Norm Strategy: (x - 50) / 50 → [-1, 1] centered at 0.

[110] oSlowD
      Meaning  : Stochastic Slow %D = 10-period SMA of oSlowK.
      Market Logic: Signal line. oSlowK crossing above oSlowD = buy signal.
      Data Type: FLOAT [0–100]
      Norm Strategy: (x - 50) / 50 → [-1, 1].

[111] RSI_03
      Meaning  : Relative Strength Index, 3-period.
                 100 - 100/(1 + RS) where RS = avg(up closes, 3) / avg(down closes, 3)
      Market Logic: Ultra-short-term momentum. RSI_03 < 30 on a 3-bar window
                    captures intra-trend pullbacks (dips to buy in uptrends).
                    RSI_03 > 70 captures short-term exhaustion.
      Data Type: FLOAT [0–100]
      Norm Strategy: (x - 50) / 50 → [-1, 1].

A15. BAR PATTERNS — Candle-level structural features
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[112] Range_CLOSE
      Meaning  : Close position within bar range.
                    +2 = close in top 25% of range (strong close)
                    +1 = close in 25%-33% from top
                     0 = middle 33%-67%
                    -1 = close in 25%-33% from bottom
                    -2 = close in bottom 25% (weak close)
      Market Logic: Where price settles matters. A +2 close (top 25%) on high
                    volume = accumulation. A -2 close on high volume = distribution.
      Data Type: INTEGER (-2 to +2)
      Norm Strategy: /2 → [-1, 1].

[113] Bar_Narrow
      Meaning  : Narrow Range bar count. Looks back up to 20 bars to find how
                 many consecutive bars had wider ranges than current.
                 Capped at 8 (NR8+), floored at 0 (if < 3 bars narrower).
                 NR7 = narrowest range in 7 bars.
      Market Logic: NR7/NR8 bars are volatility compression signals. They
                    often precede explosive breakout moves. The number encodes
                    the degree of compression.
      Data Type: INTEGER (0–8)
      Norm Strategy: /8 → [0, 1].

[114] Bar_Inside
      Meaning  : Inside bar count. Counts consecutive bars where HIGH[t] < HIGH[t-1]
                 AND LOW[t] > LOW[t-1] (current bar fully inside prior bar).
                 4 = 4 consecutive inside bars (coiling pattern).
      Market Logic: Inside bars = indecision/consolidation. Multiple inside
                    bars form a "coil" — energy building for a breakout.
                    The count signals how extended the consolidation is.
      Data Type: INTEGER (0–4)
      Norm Strategy: /4 → [0, 1].

--- Daily-only Bar Patterns ---

[115] Bar_Exhaustion  (Daily only)
      Meaning  : Exhaustion bar. +2 = open above BBH_20 AND close in bottom
                 half (bull trap / buying exhaustion). -2 = open below BBL_20
                 AND close in top half (bear trap / selling exhaustion).
      Market Logic: Wide-range reversal bars. When price opens outside the
                    Bollinger Bands but closes back toward the mean, it signals
                    that the breakout attempt failed — a potential reversal.
      Data Type: INTEGER (-2, 0, 2)
      Norm Strategy: /2 → [-1, 0, 1].

[116] Bar_Breakout    (Daily only)
      Meaning  : +1 = DayH reached 20 (new 20-bar high breakout).
                 -1 = DayL reached 20 (new 20-bar low breakdown).
      Market Logic: Breakout confirmation. A DayH=20 with Bar_Breakout=1 and
                    high volume = valid breakout. False breakouts (DayH=20 but
                    Bar_Breakout=0) are also informative.
      Data Type: INTEGER (-1, 0, 1)
      Norm Strategy: Identity.

[117] Bar_GAP_Runaway (Daily only)
      Meaning  : +1 = gap up (LOW[t] > HIGH[t-1]) in uptrend (EMA_020[t-1] < C[t-1]).
                 -1 = gap down (HIGH[t] < LOW[t-1]) in downtrend.
      Market Logic: Gap-and-run = trend continuation gaps. Price gaps beyond
                    prior range and keeps going. Strongest type of gap —
                    indicates urgency among buyers/sellers.
      Data Type: INTEGER (-1, 0, 1)
      Norm Strategy: Identity.

[118] Bar_GAP_Range   (Daily only)
      Meaning  : +1 = gap up in downtrend (counter-trend gap, likely to fill).
                 -1 = gap down in uptrend (counter-trend gap, likely to fill)
                 AND DayH/DayL < 20 (not a breakout).
      Market Logic: Gap into range = exhaustion/fade gap. Price gaps but the
                    prior trend and Day indicator suggest the gap will fill.
                    These are fade-trading opportunities.
      Data Type: INTEGER (-1, 0, 1)
      Norm Strategy: Identity.

[124] BottomTop
      Meaning  : Composite pattern label string. Concatenation of sub-patterns
                 detected by the trend engine:
                   /+BTM  = Bull Trend Start (beginning of uptrend)
                   /+Mid  = Bull Trend Middle (confirmed uptrend continuation)
                   /+Beauty= Beauty Trend (strong trend with tight channels)
                   /+Spring= Spring pattern (bear trap → bull reversal)
                   /+Pivot = Pivot (trend acceleration within existing uptrend)
                   /+PDF   = Price Discovery / Follow-through
                   /-DWN   = Bearish breakdown (downtrend initiation)
                   /-CRH   = Bearish crash signal
                   /+Bull  = Bull confirmation with SPY alignment
                   /-Bear  = Bear confirmation with SPY alignment
      Market Logic: This is the output of a sophisticated multi-condition
                    pattern recognition system. Each sub-pattern combines
                    trend (SMA/EMA alignment), momentum (MACD, Push_Diff,
                    TTM_Momentum), structural (Trend_PV, 3Bar, PV phases),
                    and confirmation (Stochastic, BIAS_Diff) checks.
                    The specific label encodes the regime type and quality.
      Data Type: STRING (e.g., "/+BTM/+Mid", "/+Beauty", "")
      Norm Strategy: Multi-hot encoding or learned embedding. Each sub-label
                    (BTM, Mid, Beauty, Spring, Pivot, PDF, DWN, CRH, Bull,
                    Bear) becomes a binary feature.

      Sub-pattern definitions (from the EasyLanguage code):

      /+BTM (Bottom — trend initiation):
        Bull_CNT 1-4 + Stochastic_Bull_CNT=1 + positive momentum across all
        oscillators (TTM, MACD, Push_Diff) + close > open.
        OR: Bull_CNT=1 + existing Stochastic bull + brief prior bear phase
        (1-2 bars) + SMA_050 rising + all oscillators positive + 3Bar > 0 + PV > 0.

      /+Mid (Middle — trend continuation):
        SMA_020 > SMA_050 > SMA_200 (bull alignment), SMA_200 rising, PV > 0,
        MACD histogram growing, recent Push_Diff weakness (3-bar dip), and a
        trigger event (Push crosses 0, 3Bar flips positive, PV flips, or Bull_CNT=1).
        OR: SMA_020 < SMA_050 but SMA_050 > SMA_200 (pullback within uptrend),
        with stronger confirmation required (TTM momentum turning, Push crossing 0).

      /+Beauty (Beauty trend — strong, clean uptrend):
        C > EMA_020 > EMA_050 > EMA_200, MACD_DIFF > 0, Push_Diff > 0,
        Trend_PV < 3 (early phase), 5-bar confirmation that price rides above
        Trigger (tight channel), Trigger < SMA_020 + 1.5*STD.

      /+Spring (Spring — bear trap reversal):
        C > EMA_020, EMA_050 > EMA_200, EMAs compressing, Trend_PV = -1
        (brief bear phase), close > prior close, PV_BEAR_START declining
        (lower highs on bear phases = compression), and oscillator turns
        (Stochastic %K curling, RSI_03 curling, or Trigger cross).

      /+Pivot (Pivot — trend acceleration):
        C > SMA_200, Trend_PV rising (+1 to +N), Bull/Bear composite is bearish
        (contrarian), Stochastic_Bull active, Trigger near SMA_020+STD,
        with MA slope or Stochastic or Push_Diff accelerating,
        strong close (2*(C-O) > H-L or close near top), and no bearish divergence.

      /+PDF (Price Discovery Follow-through):
        DayCH breakout (DayCH > 2*prior DayCH, >5), 3Bar > 0, PV in early
        bull phase (1-2), MACD histogram growing and above signal, MA alignment
        (either SMA_020 > SMA_050, or SMA_020 < SMA_050 with SMA_050 > SMA_200,
        or C > SMA_050 in downtrend).
        OR: SMA_020 > SMA_050, MACD leading price (MACD bottom predates price
        bottom by 1.5x), and at least one oscillator turning positive.

      /-DWN (Down — bearish breakdown):
        Three scenarios of increasing severity, all requiring: O > C, C[t-1] > C,
        and a filter excluding BBL_20 above SMA_050 and BIAS_Diff/TTM positive.
        Scenario 1 (SMA_050 < SMA_020, SMA_200 > SMA_050): strong close weakness
        (O-C > 3*(C-L)), SMA_020 diverging from SMA_050, Trend_PV = -1, all
        oscillators negative (BIAS_Diff/TTM/TTM_Momentum/MACD_DIFF/Push_Diff < 0).
        Scenario 2 (SMA_050 < SMA_020, SMA_200 > SMA_020): price rejected at
        SMA_050/Trigger, with multiple exclusion filters for false signals.
        Scenario 3 (SMA_050 < SMA_020, SMA_200 < SMA_050): highest severity,
        requires history of SMA_050 < SMA_200 (bear market), SMA_020[2] > SMA_020[1]
        (failed rally), trend destruction across multiple dimensions.

      /-CRH (Crash — accelerated bearish breakdown):
        SMA_050 > SMA_020 (bear cross), SMA_050 declining from peak, price
        below all MAs and Trigger, 20-bar SMA_050 peak > 5 bars ago, PV < 2
        (bear phase starting), recent price above SMA_020 (trapped longs),
        Push_Diff declining, BBH[t-1] + 2*STD > SMA_200.
        OR: same SMA configuration but even weaker — price below all MAs,
        MACD/TTM negative, 3Bar negative, BBL below SMA_200 or extreme.

      /+Bull (Bull confirmation with SPY alignment):
        Trend_PV = 1 (new bull phase) AND one of:
        - Bull_CNT = 1, SPY also in bull, Stochastic_Bull_SPY >= 2,
          Stochastic_Bear_SPY = 0, EMAs not in bearish alignment.
        - Bull_CNT = 1, SPY Bull_CNT > 0, Stochastic_Bull_SPY = 1,
          Stochastic_Bear_SPY = 0, EMA_050 rising.
        - Bull_CNT = 1, SPY Bull_CNT > 1, Stochastic bull with tighter
          or equal count to SPY, SPY Stochastic bull > bear.
        - Bull_CNT > 1, SPY Bull_CNT > 0, BIAS_Diff curling up (trough
          pattern: [2]>0, [1]<0, [0]>0), all oscillators positive, EMAs
          aligned bull (EMA_200 < EMA_050 < EMA_020), Trigger near band.

      /-Bear (Bear confirmation with SPY alignment):
        Trend_PV = -1, C < EMA_200 (bear market), SPY_050 > SPY_L (SPY not
        at extreme), bear counts align with or exceed SPY, and recent high
        above EMA_020 (failed rally), with exclusion filters for premature
        signals.

C2. VIX — Volatility Indices
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

     VIX indices measure implied volatility from SPX option prices.

[006] VIX_9D
      Meaning  : 9-day VIX (short-term implied volatility).
      Market Logic: "Fear gauge" for very near-term. Spikes on sudden
                    market drops. Mean-reverting: high VIX → future low
                    VIX (and market recovery).
      Data Type: FLOAT (typically 8–85, extreme ~100+)
      Norm Strategy: Log(x) then Z-score (VIX is log-normal).

[007] VIX_3M
      Meaning  : 3-month VIX (medium-term IV).
      Market Logic: Less reactive than VIX_9D. The spread between VIX_9D
                    and VIX_3M is the VIX futures curve front slope.
      Data Type: FLOAT (typically 10–50)
      Norm Strategy: Log(x) then Z-score.

[008] VIX_6M
      Meaning  : 6-month VIX (longer-term IV expectation).
      Market Logic: Smoothest VIX measure. Used for back slope of term
                    structure.
      Data Type: FLOAT (typically 12–40)
      Norm Strategy: Log(x) then Z-score.

[009] VIX_Front_Slope
      Meaning  : VIX_3M - VIX_9D.
      Market Logic: Positive = contango (normal market — near-term fear
                    lower than medium-term). Negative = backwardation
                    (stress — near-term fear exceeds medium-term). A flip
                    to backwardation often marks a market bottom.
      Data Type: FLOAT (typically -15 to +10)
      Norm Strategy: Z-score.

[010] VIX_Back_Slope
      Meaning  : VIX_6M - VIX_3M.
      Market Logic: Measures the steepness of the far end of the VIX curve.
                    Persistent flattening = complacency.
      Data Type: FLOAT (typically -5 to +5)
      Norm Strategy: Z-score.

[011] VIX_9D_Z
      Meaning  : Z-score of VIX_9D relative to 200-day MA.
                (VIX_9D - AVG(VIX_9D, 200)) / STDDEV(VIX_9D, 200)
      Market Logic: How extreme is current fear relative to the last 200 days?
                    Z > 2 = extremely fearful (potential bounce).
                    Z < -1 = extremely complacent (potential correction).
      Data Type: FLOAT (typically -2 to +4)
      Norm Strategy: Already a Z-score — Identity or clip to [-3, 3].

[012] VIX_3M_Z
      Meaning  : Z-score of VIX_3M relative to 200-day MA.
      Data Type: FLOAT
      Norm Strategy: Identity (already normalized).

C4. MARKET INTERNALS — By Index (SPY, QQQ, RSL, NYSE)
================================================================================

     For each of four indices (S&P 500, NASDAQ-100, Russell 2000, NYSE),
     the following 6 derived indicators are computed from raw Advance/Decline/
     UpVolume/DownVolume/NewHigh/NewLow data.

     Raw inputs:
       ADV  = number of advancing issues
       DEC  = number of declining issues
       UVL  = up volume (volume of advancing issues)
       DVL  = down volume (volume of declining issues)
       NH   = new 52-week highs
       NL   = new 52-week lows

--- For S&P 500 (columns 17–22) ---

[017] TRIN_Z_SPY
      Meaning  : Z-score of Arms Index (TRIN).
                 TRIN = (ADV/DEC) / (UVL/DVL)  [advance/decline ratio
                        divided by up/down volume ratio].
                 Then Z-scored over 200 days.
      Market Logic: TRIN < 1 = bullish (volume flowing into advancing stocks).
                    TRIN > 1 = bearish (volume flowing into declining stocks).
                    Z-score normalizes across regimes. TRIN_Z < -1.5 = extreme
                    bullish volume flow.
      Data Type: FLOAT
      Norm Strategy: Identity (already Z-scored).

[018] NHNL_Ratio_SPY
      Meaning  : NH / (NH + NL).
      Market Logic: 1.00 = all new highs (extremely bullish). 0.00 = all new
                    lows (extremely bearish). 0.50 = balance. This is the
                    purest measure of trend health — are stocks making new
                    highs or new lows?
      Data Type: FLOAT [0–1]
      Norm Strategy: (x - 0.5) * 2 → [-1, 1].

[019] Fosback_SPY
      Meaning  : Fosback Index = MIN(NH, NL) / Median(ADV+DEC, 5).
      Market Logic: Norman Fosback's indicator. High values = many new highs
                    AND many new lows simultaneously (divergence/churn).
                    Low values = one-sided market (either strongly up or
                    strongly down). A spike in Fosback often precedes a top.
      Data Type: FLOAT [0–1]
      Norm Strategy: Z-score.

[020] Participation_SPY
      Meaning  : (NH - NL) / Median(ADV+DEC, 5).
      Market Logic: Net new highs as % of total issues. Positive = more new
                    highs (broad participation). Negative = more new lows
                    (broad deterioration). The 5-day median denominator
                    smooths total issues for comparability.
      Data Type: FLOAT (can be negative)
      Norm Strategy: Z-score.

[021] Advance_SPY
      Meaning  : ADV / Median(ADV+DEC, 5).
      Market Logic: % of issues advancing. > 0.50 = more advancers.
                    A day with Advance > 0.70 = broad rally.
      Data Type: FLOAT [0–1]
      Norm Strategy: (x - 0.5) * 2 → [-1, 1].

[022] UpVolume_SPY
      Meaning  : UVL / Median(UVL+DVL, 5).
      Market Logic: % of volume in advancing stocks. > 0.50 = volume favors
                    advancers. UpVolume > 0.80 on an up day = strong
                    institutional buying.
      Data Type: FLOAT [0–1]
      Norm Strategy: (x - 0.5) * 2 → [-1, 1].

================================================================================
E. NORMALIZATION STRATEGY SUMMARY
================================================================================

For the Transformer Encoder, all features must be normalized to comparable
scales before being projected into the 768-dim embedding space. Below is the
recommended strategy by feature category.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Category              | Strategy            | Rationale
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Raw prices (O,H,L,C)  | Z-score per ticker  | Removes absolute price level;
                       |   or log-return     | model sees relative changes
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SPY-relative prices   | Z-score             | Already cross-sectionally
                       |                     | comparable
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HA prices             | Z-score             | Smooth variant of raw prices
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Volume                | Log(1+x) → Z-score  | Volume is log-normal
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SMA/EMA               | (MA - CLOSE)/CLOSE  | Distance from MA as % of price
                       |   then Z-score      | is more informative than raw MA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Bollinger Bands       | (CLOSE - BB)/STD    | Already a normalized distance;
                       |                     | Identity or light Z-score
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ATR, STD_20, TTM      | /CLOSE → Z-score    | Relative to price level
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RSI, Stochastic       | (x - 50)/50         | Center at 0, range [-1, 1]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PFE                   | (x - 50)/50         | Center at 0, range [-1, 1]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Integer states        | Identity or /max    | Already compact. E.g.:
  (-2 to +2)          |                     | Trend_S_HKA / 2 → [-1, 1]
  (0 to N)            |                     | Log(1+x) for duration counters
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Binary flags          | Identity            | Already 0/1 or -1/0/1
  (Trend_BEAR_BULL,   |                     |
   PV_BULL, etc.)     |                     |
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VIX & VIX slopes      | Log(VIX) → Z-score  | VIX is log-normal
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Breadth % (SP_Above,  | (x - 50)/50         | Range [-1, 1]
  Advance, UpVolume)  |                     |
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NHNL_Ratio            | (x - 0.5)*2         | Range [-1, 1]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TRIN_Z, VIX_Z         | Identity            | Already Z-scored in formula
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Growth_vs_Value       | Z-score             | Ratio, unbounded
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BottomTop             | Multi-hot           | Each sub-label → binary feature
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

================================================================================
IMPLEMENTATION NOTES
================================================================================

1. TICKER-WISE FITTING: Z-score normalization should be fit per-ticker (or
   per-ticker-group) for price-level features, but cross-sectional (across
   all tickers) for market features and SPY-relative features.

2. FORWARD-FILL NOTE: When merging daily + weekly + monthly timeframes into a
   single sequence, weekly and monthly features are forward-filled to daily
   frequency. During forward-fill, normalized values should be filled, not
   raw values (to avoid introducing scale artifacts when a monthly bar spans
   ~21 daily rows).

3. SEQUENCE CONSTRUCTION: Each input token to the Transformer is a concatenated
   vector of [MKT features, Daily features, Weekly features, Monthly features]
   for a given date. The weekly/monthly portions are forward-filled from the
   most recent weekly/monthly bar.

4. MISSING DATA: Early bars in weekly/monthly datasets may have fewer features
   (e.g., no long-period MAs). These should be masked (set to 0 and flagged
   with an attention mask) rather than imputed.

5. PRINCIPLE COMPLIANCE:
   - Features marked "structural" (Trend_PV, Trend_S_3Bar, DayH/L, HA candles)
     preserve market microstructure (Principle #4).
   - SPY counterparts for most features provide market context (Principle #5).
   - No pre-digested trading rules are present as raw features — the BottomTop
     labels are the closest, and they are encoded as multi-hot binary flags
     rather than a single "buy/sell" signal (Principle #2).
   - Redundant moving averages (13 SMAs) may benefit from dimensionality
     reduction (PCA or learned projection) if the Transformer input dimension
     becomes a bottleneck (Principle #1).
