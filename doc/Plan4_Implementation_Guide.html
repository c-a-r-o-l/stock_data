0MAIN IDEA

What this project is

This is exploratory, interpretable research — not a trading system, not a prediction machine, and not a claim of found alpha. The question:

Under which market regimes, for companies in which financial states, showing which price structures, do stocks tend to perform well relative to their peers — and is that regularity real, measurable, and stable enough to be trusted out of sample?

"Conditional structure" means: the relationship between a stock's characteristics and its future returns is not fixed — it changes with the market environment — and that change can be quantified. Not "I can predict returns," but "the rules governing returns shift with regime, and I can show when and by how much."

The three axes and the pipeline

Three independent, crossed condition axes (crossed, not nested — they take values independently, and the statistics cross them; merging or nesting them would destroy the interaction being studied):

price series  → personal indicator → structure state  ┐   (stock, day)      two-layer
market data   → clustering         → regime state      ├─→   table      →   Fama-MacBeth
fundamentals  → clustering         → financial state   ┘   + rank label     B0→B1→B2→B3

One row per (stock, day). Example row: (AAPL, 2018-03-15) → regime #2 "calm growth" · financial cluster #1 "profitable compounder" · structure intact, leg 3, +12% above leg origin · label 0.83 — meaning AAPL's beta-residualized forward 3-month return beat 83% of the universe that day.

Why two axes are clustered and one is hand-encoded: clustering is what you do when you have no theory and want the data to sort itself (no strong prior on what the market's "seasons" are). For price structure you do have a theory — the indicator — so you impose it rather than letting an algorithm grope for it. The price data is never clustered; the indicator is the third axis. There is no fourth ingredient. Raw price paths are never fed in directly: they aren't comparable across stocks and eras, and learning a representation from them is the path this project deliberately killed — effective sample size can't support it. Everything enters pre-digested into a small number of nameable states.

The governing constraint: effective sample size

Row counts lie. Forward label windows overlap (~62 of 63 days shared between adjacent days) and the regime is shared market-wide, so the number of genuinely independent observations is tiny. A regime that occurred ~4 times in history offers ~4 independent pieces of evidence about itself, no matter how many trading days or stocks it spans. Like restaurant reviews: 1,000 reviews from your own 3 visits are 3 opinions, not 1,000. Every major design choice below — coarse discrete buckets, a 3-month horizon, two-way interactions as primary, episode-level counting — is sized to this ceiling. It is a physical limit; nothing can conjure more recessions.

What success and failure look like

A positive finding: "High-leverage cyclicals earn top-quartile cross-sectional ranks in expansion regimes but bottom-quartile in risk-off regimes; the effect held in the 2000s and 2010s slices, survived multiple-testing correction, and confirmed on held-out data. Structure states added incremental conditioning power beyond regime and fundamentals."

A null finding is also a finding: "Once effective sample size and multiple testing were accounted for, no conditional pattern survived confirmation; the apparent regime-dependence was noise." That is a defensible research result. For a prediction machine, a null result is just a machine that doesn't work. This asymmetry is the design's real cleverness — it is why the exploratory framing was chosen (D1) and why the project cannot be dismissed as "fancy models hiding that nothing was found."

Framing analogy

Nutrition research, not diet pills. The pill seller promises weight loss; the researcher says "among people who don't exercise much, cutting refined sugar is associated with weight loss, and this held in three separate cohorts." More cautious, harder to knock down — and it's the latter that interviewers trust.

Positioning: what is and isn't novel

The skeleton is not novel, and an interviewer will recognize it immediately: conditional factor models go back to Ferson–Harvey (early 1990s); regime-switching return models to Hamilton (1989); "value works in some environments and not others" has a whole practitioner literature (AQR, Man Group publish regime-conditioned factor performance); several funds have publicly blogged clustering macro data into 4–6 regimes. Fama-MacBeth is the workhorse of half of empirical asset pricing. That is fine, arguably good: nobody expects a student summer project to discover alpha; they expect craft, and a well-trodden framework lets the methodology be judged against a known standard.

Two things are yours: the path-dependent structure indicator (nobody has tested that specific state variable in this framework, and it is treated as a hypothesis to falsify, not a premise) and the rigor discipline. The one-line positioning: the framework is standard; the indicator and the honesty about effective sample size are mine. Don't sell novelty.

Resolving "isn't this obvious?": the hypothesis ("relationships depend on regime") is obvious; the answer is not. Which combinations flip? By how many percentile points? Did the flip survive 2008? How many apparent patterns die after correction? (Empirically: most.) "Exercise is good for you" is obvious; a dose-response study with confidence intervals is science. The quantified, correction-surviving, out-of-sample-confirmed map is the non-obvious deliverable.

Why the rigor is the point: the replication crisis

The central controversy of empirical finance's last decade. Academics published a "zoo" of 300+ anomalies. Hou, Xue & Zhang replicated 447 with consistent methods: 64% insignificant at the 5% level; 85% insignificant at the stricter t≈3 bar recommended by Harvey–Liu–Zhu, who concluded "most claimed research findings in financial economics are likely false." Diagnosed causes: p-hacking, publication bias, thousands of researchers testing thousands of characteristics with nobody correcting for the combinations tried across the whole field. The debate isn't settled — Jensen et al. (2023, J. Finance) argue ~75–82% replicate under a Bayesian treatment — but every side agrees the field got burned by multiple testing.

Scanning many (regime × financial × structure) cells is structurally the same activity that produced the zoo. That is why the guardrails — counting tests, sealed confirmation, effective-N counting — are not polish; they are the machinery separating a real conditional effect from a coin that landed heads eight times because 500 coins were flipped. The fact that obvious-sounding regularities fail this often is exactly why "I tested honestly and here is what survived" has value.

1DECISIONS

Decision ledger — every major choice, with its history

Each entry: what we previously planned, why it was wrong or fragile, what we do now, and why. Reasoning lives here; the how-to lives in §3 and cross-references these IDs. Entries tagged Settled #n correspond to the six explicitly settled decisions from the working discussion.

D1 · Project framing
Previously

A prediction machine: hand-built factor vectors ("Vector A") indexed in FAISS; k-NN over nearest historical analogues, upgraded to XGBoost calibrated probabilities; purged cost-aware walk-forward backtest reporting Sharpe/Sortino/drawdown; a Transformer encoder ("Vector B") as challenger.

Problem

It ultimately promised a Sharpe ratio — the exact number it admitted rests on ~20 effective independent observations. To top quant firms a "prediction project stacked with trendy components" is a negative: they see hundreds yearly and suspect nothing real inside. And a null result is just a machine that doesn't work.

Now

An exploratory study of conditional structure: two-layer Fama-MacBeth on regime × financial-state × structure interactions against forward cross-sectional rank. No backtest, no accuracy metrics, no tradability claim (explicitly out of scope). Retrieval demoted to optional visualization; encoder to stretch goal; RL rejected.

Why

Continues the old plan's own thesis — "rigor beats architecture" — far enough to delete the architecture. Success ("is the conditional regularity stable?") becomes a claim the data can actually support, and a null result becomes a defensible finding. The traded-away desk story ("did the sophisticated thing beat the simple thing net of costs") is answered in interviews with a deliberate "out of scope" (§6).

D2 · The label Settled #1
Previously

v1: top 10% by total 3-month return. v2 (the old plan's "fix"): excess return relative to the market. v3: cross-sectional rank of raw return.

Problem

v1: total return contains beta — in a bull market top gainers are just the most market-sensitive names, so "good stock" collapses into "bet on market direction." v2 is a no-op under a rank label: subtracting the same market return from every stock changes no ranks — rank(rᵢ) = rank(rᵢ − r_m) identically. v3 strips the common level but not the beta channel, because the beta problem lives in the ordering: high-beta names rank top in up-markets and bottom in down-markets regardless. Without a real fix, "conditional structure" risks rediscovering "beta works in bull regimes." (The v2 error survived in earlier notes; this entry is the corrected canonical version.)

Now

Beta-residualized rank: label = cross-sectional percentile rank of rᵢ − βᵢ·r_m over the forward 63-trading-day window, ranked within the universe each day. βᵢ estimated point-in-time (trailing 252 days, only data before t), winsorized cross-sectionally at the 1st/99th percentiles, shrunk toward 1 (Blume: β* = 0.67β̂ + 0.33). Additionally, β̂ enters layer-1 as a control regressor as a robustness check (residual beta catch).

Why

Removes the beta ordering channel while keeping the rank label's virtues: naturally relative to the peer universe (in a raging bull market every raw return may be +20%, but ranks still separate winners from losers) and robust to level shifts across eras. Point-in-time estimation avoids look-ahead (a full-sample beta is leakage of the classic full-sample-normalization kind); shrinkage/winsorization stop noisy single-stock betas from dominating the residuals. The 252-day window is an arbitrary constant → it joins the perturbation-check list (D12's principle applied beyond the indicator).

D3 · Universe & survivorship Settled #2 · #3
Previously

Plan text: "universe fixed, including delisted companies" — with the clean version living in CRSP/Compustat-tier subscriptions or student-priced Sharadar/Norgate. A middle path was considered: point-in-time S&P 500 / Nasdaq-100 index membership reconstructed from public constituent-change histories ("in universe on day t iff in the index on day t"), which recovers removed-but-not-delisted names nearly for free.

Problem

Buying delisted-inclusive data: cost and effort disproportionate to a resume/interview project. Point-in-time membership: judged too much wrangling for the payoff by the project owner. Meanwhile "fixed universe" was underspecified everywhere — a list fixed at any single date is itself selection (today's names = survivorship even with delisted data; a 2000 snapshot excludes later IPOs).

Now

Deliberately accept the bias: universe = the current ~277 stocks in hand (today's S&P 500 / Nasdaq-100 large caps with fundamentals; 313 clean-tech symbols minus 36 ETFs). Do not buy data; do not reconstruct membership. In the write-up and interviews, sign the bias, don't just name it.

Why

Scope realism beats completeness for this project's purpose. The bias is real and non-uniform: it concentrates in exactly the cells studied — firms delisted through bankruptcy are disproportionately high-leverage cyclicals in risk-off regimes, so those cells' returns are upward-biased precisely where conditional structure is most interesting. The rank label mitigates market-level survivorship (everyone is ranked against each other) but not this within-cross-section distortion. Large-cap restriction is itself the standard mitigation (bankruptcy delisting is rarest there). The bias also grows with lookback distance: the 2001 cross-section of today's constituents is disproportionately future winners. The signed statement — "stress-regime and high-leverage cells are upward-biased because the casualties are missing, and the bias worsens in early years" — reads as understanding; an unsigned disclaimer reads as a checkbox. Have the "what would fix it" answer ready (CRSP/Sharadar/Norgate, or point-in-time membership — considered, declined for scope).

D4 · Discrete states, not a continuous model
Previously

Two continuous temptations: (a) a continuous interaction surface — coefficients on VIX × leverage × structure-distance, or a boosted tree learning interactions; (b) in the old plan, "discretize" meant tokenizing factors to feed a sequence model (verdict there: don't — a different question, and the old warning "enter regime features as raw magnitudes, not binarized" was about distance metrics for retrieval, not conditioning; superficially contradictory, actually consistent).

Problem

The research object is an interaction: "does the effect of X flip across regimes?" With ~4–6 truly independent episodes per rare regime, a continuous surface has essentially nothing pinning it down. It also tests implicitly infinitely many hypotheses (no countable correction) and has no clean stability check.

Now

Coarse discrete buckets as the primary design: 4–6 regimes, ~3–4 financial states, ~3 structure states. "Controlled fuzziness" — the honest name for deliberate variance reduction sized to the data. Continuous Fama-MacBeth interaction terms kept as a secondary robustness check: if an effect shows up in discrete cells but the continuous interaction coefficient is zero, suspect an artifact of an arbitrary bin boundary.

Why

Discreteness buys three rigor properties: a countable hypothesis set ("we tested N cells, here's the correction"), a well-defined stability check (bootstrap the clustering, see if the partition survives), and nameable conditions you can count independent episodes of. Honest costs, stated openly: boundaries are arbitrary, edge observations flip clusters, results can be sensitive to cluster count — which is exactly why the stability bootstrap and perturbation checks exist. Also keep two "not enough data" problems distinct: too many features per row (curse of dimensionality — fewer features helps) vs. too few independent observations (effective N — nothing helps). Coarse bucketing does not manufacture recessions; it rations scarce evidence into a few well-populated claims instead of fragmenting it into hundreds of near-empty cells. The defensible line: "discrete as primary because effective N demands it and interpretability is the deliverable; continuous as a check" — never "continuous is impossible."

D5 · The measurement: two-layer Fama-MacBeth, correctly specified
Previously

Stage C wording: layer one estimates the effect of a full "(regime × financial × structure) combination" within each single day. Alternative also considered: one giant pooled regression over all rows.

Problem

The regime is constant within a day (shared market-wide), so a within-day cross-sectional regression cannot estimate a regime effect — it is absorbed into the day's intercept. The wording described an impossible regression. The pooled alternative is worse: on 2020-03-16 all 200 stocks moved together — 200 rows, roughly one piece of evidence about crash days. A day, not a row, is the unit of evidence.

Now

Layer 1 — within each day t, regress the label rank on financial-state × structure-state cell dummies (equivalently: cell mean minus grand mean), plus β̂ as control (D2). Output: one small vector of effect estimates per day. Everything shared market-wide that day (direction, sentiment, the regime itself) cancels because every stock experienced it equally. Layer 2 — treat the daily estimates as a time series: overall mean and consistency; then group the days by the regime prevailing at t and compare average effects across regimes. That cross-regime difference is the conditional structure. The regime is never a stock feature; it is the grouping variable for daily estimates.

Why

Respects the cross-sectional label, respects the unit of evidence, and puts the regime where it is estimable. Conditioning is on the regime at formation date t — if the regime changes during the 63-day label window, that is part of what "regime at t predicts what follows" means, and episode-level aggregation (D6) handles the dependence. Layer 2 is where "stable enough to trust" stops being a vibe and becomes a number.

D6 · Inference that matches the effective sample
Previously

Naive layer-2 statistics: mean of daily estimates, standard error with N = number of days. The old plan had the overlap-correction instinct (Newey-West / non-overlapping / block bootstrap); the newer plan kept the effective-N concept but dropped it from the procedure.

Problem

With a 63-day horizon sampled daily, β(Monday) and β(Tuesday) share ~62 of 63 label days — nearly the same measurement twice. Naive SEs overstate significance by a large factor. Effective N ≈ calendar span ÷ horizon, not day count.

Now

Newey-West standard errors with ~63 lags on all layer-2 series (alternatives: sample every 63rd day; block-bootstrap the coefficient series — the joint bootstrap of D10 provides this anyway). Regime-conditional claims counted in independent episodes, not days: an episode = a maximal contiguous run of one regime label (runs < 10 trading days flagged as "transition" and excluded from episode counts). Minimum-evidence rule fixed in advance: no conclusion for any cell/contrast with fewer than 4 episodes spanning fewer than 3 calendar years — below threshold is reported as "insufficient data," never forced. Horizon fixed at a single H = 63 trading days (~3 months): a decade holds ~40 non-overlapping 3-month windows vs. ~10 annual ones, and independent evidence is the scarcest resource. Multiple horizons are an optional extension only.

Why

The same effective-N number from the concept section must reappear inside the machinery, or the machinery is lying. Refusing to conclude below threshold is the project's honesty rule; it is enforced by code, not intention.

D7 · The cell-count collision: two-way primary, three-way exploratory
Previously

Headline object: the full three-way map — every (regime × financial × structure) cell characterized. 4–6 × ~4 × ~3–4 ≈ 50–100 cells.

Problem

Total independent evidence across the entire regime axis is maybe 20–30 episodes; a rare regime ("panic": 2008, March 2020, maybe 2011, late 2018) offers ~4 episodes — and more stocks don't help, because every stock shares the same episode. Spread 20–30 episodes over 50–100 cells and most cells get well under one episode of clean evidence, so the project's own minimum-episode rule (D6) stamps the majority of the headline object "insufficient data." Two individually-correct safeguards, multiplied, constrain the headline goal — and the cells that would show the most interesting flips (crash cells) are exactly the thinnest. That is the structure of the problem, not a bug. Better known in week one than week nine.

Now

(1) Two-way interactions are the primary results — "does the fundamentals→return relationship differ between regimes?", with the option of pooling to two coarse super-regimes (risk-on / risk-off) where episode counts demand it. Two-way beats three-way; coarse beats fine; this is what controlled fuzziness is for — survival, not elegance. (2) The three-way cross is exploratory, plus a pre-registered set of ≤ 5 specific three-way hypotheses (five, not five hundred) so correction doesn't annihilate everything. (3) The honest catalogue — "we attempted the full map; here are the cells with enough episodes, and the 90+ that did not clear the bar" — is itself a legitimate, even impressive deliverable that directly demonstrates understanding of effective N. Make peace in advance with it feeling different from "I mapped the full conditional structure." (4) Lengthening history to buy episodes was considered and declined: it drags in worse survivorship bias (D3) and more Drift B (D14) — a trade, not a free win. Partial pooling (hierarchical shrinkage of three-way cells toward two-way margins) is an optional extension.

Why

Right-sizes the promise to the physics. The evidence budget funds a few well-populated claims or hundreds of noisy ones; choose the former on purpose.

D8 · Validation: expanding-window hybrid, not a single sealed block
Previously

One contiguous never-touched stretch of time as the sealed final exam, opened once.

Problem

The sealed block contains only the regimes that happened during it — so the project's only hard boundary against overfitting is structurally unable to confirm claims about regimes absent from the holdout (direct collision with the effective-N ceiling). The single block also can't examine most of the syllabus.

Now

Expanding-window walk-forward, hybrid form: train 2000→X, test X+1; extend; repeat. Iterate freely on early folds (validation years); seal the later folds as the true final exam, run once. Four conditions, all mandatory: (1) Preserve "touched once" — if you run sealed folds, look, tweak, rerun, every test year silently became development data; the hybrid split exists precisely so iteration happens on validation folds only. (2) Embargo at every boundary — labels look 63 trading days forward, so purge the last 63 trading days from each training window (train through ~September to test January onward). The same purge gap applies between the exploration period and confirmation folds. (3) Fix the cluster rule up front — freeze regime/financial cluster definitions (feature spec + scaler statistics + centroids) on the first training window and apply forward, so "regime 3" means the same thing in every year. (Refit-per-fold with centroid matching is the inferior alternative; freezing is cleaner and matches exploration/confirmation separation.) (4) The physical ceiling stays — walk-forward stops you wasting episodes but creates none; aggregate across folds by episode, not by year or day, and expect early folds to be noisy. Default boundaries (OQ6): initial train 2000-07→2011-12 (dot-com bust, GFC, 2011); validation folds 2012→2019; sealed 2020-01→present (COVID crash, 2022 inflation bear, 2023+ bull — good regime coverage for confirmation). For regimes still absent from sealed folds, fall back to era-sliced consistency (Drift B machinery, D14), explicitly labeled as weaker evidence.

Why

Every year after the initial block becomes an out-of-sample test year, so every regime episode after ~2011 gets tested on data the classifications never saw — the coverage problem largely solved without pretending the ceiling moved.

D9 · Frozen clusters need an escape hatch: the "unclassified" state Settled #4
Previously

Freeze regime definitions on the first training window (D8 condition 3) and force-assign every later day to the nearest centroid.

Problem

A regime type absent from 2000–2011 — e.g., a 2022-style "fighting inflation" episode has no real analogue in that window — gets silently mapped to whichever old centroid is least wrong. Relative features (D14, Drift A) reduce but don't eliminate genuinely new configurations.

Now

A distance-to-centroid threshold, frozen in advance so it isn't a new researcher degree of freedom: take the 95th percentile of within-cluster distances on the first training window; any later day beyond it becomes an explicit "unclassified" regime state — reported, never silently assigned. The 95 is an arbitrary constant → perturbation list (90 / 95 / 97.5).

Why

Two side benefits beyond correctness: the firing rate is itself a diagnostic (if 30% of post-2021 days are unclassified, that is a drift finding, not a nuisance), and it gives a clean answer to the interview question "what does your model do when it sees something new?" — it says so, out loud, instead of pretending.

D10 · Uncertainty propagation: one joint bootstrap, not two disconnected ones Settled #5
Previously

Two separate bootstraps: one to certify cluster stability, one for effect confidence intervals — with the regime label treated as known truth in the second.

Problem

The regime label attached to each day is an estimated quantity. Days near cluster boundaries flipping assignment would move the cross-regime comparisons that are the headline result, and the disconnected design never lets that uncertainty reach the final numbers. Separately: any bootstrap here must be a block bootstrap. Market days aren't independent (today ≈ yesterday; labels overlap 62/63), so resampling days i.i.d. produces near-identical pseudo-histories and cheerfully certifies everything as "stable" and "significant" — the restaurant-review problem in disguise.

Now

Joint pipeline block bootstrap, ~500 resamples: draw contiguous multi-month blocks (default length 126 trading days; alternatives 63 / 252 / whole-episode blocks on the perturbation list) with replacement → refit clusters on the resample → match resampled clusters to the original partition by centroid similarity (Hungarian assignment; greedy acceptable) → reassign days → rerun layers 1 and 2 → collect effects. Report CIs from the spread and the assignment-instability rate. Resamples where matching fails badly are informative, not discarded — they are the cluster-instability signal showing up inside the CI machinery, which is the point of doing it jointly. Acceptable simplification, stated honestly if used: hold trailing-feature construction fixed and block-resample the day-level feature/label panel.

Why

Some pseudo-histories omit 2008, others contain it twice — that variety is the honest answer to "what if history had gone differently." Cost: full pipeline × ~500 ≈ hours, embarrassingly parallel (each resample independent); never run inside the development iteration loop.

D11 · Clustering methodology
Previously

Let the algorithm fully automatically decide the number of clusters — "most objective." Also, at one point, cluster financials and macro together in one step.

Problem

Automatic k just hides the subjectivity inside the choice of criterion — different criteria give very different counts. Merging the clusterings destroys the research object: studying "how the effect of financials changes with regime" requires financials and regime to be independent axes that can be crossed; merged, "high leverage" is no longer a fixed coordinate comparable across regimes. Additionally, financial-state clustering on raw ratios will partly rediscover sectors (a bank's leverage ≠ an industrial's), so "high-leverage cyclicals underperform in regime X" may be "financials underperform in regime X" in disguise.

Now

Separate clusterings, crossed later in the statistics. k-means (GMM as alternative) with the algorithm suggesting a range — regimes k ∈ {4,5,6}, financial states k ∈ {3,4,5} — settled by stability + interpretability: block-bootstrap the clustering and keep only partitions that re-form under resampling (adjusted Rand index across resamples); merge or drop unstable clusters; every kept cluster must be nameable in one line ("panic / flight to safety," "profitable steady compounder"). Deliberately small k (controlled fuzziness, D4/D7). Sector confound: handled per OQ3/OQ4 — sector-relative features if a sector mapping is acquired, otherwise the confound is stated in the write-up.

Why

"Stable under reshuffling and explainable" is a sturdier standard than any single mathematical criterion, and interpretable clusters are themselves a deliverable (the report names them).

D12 · The personal indicator: role, contamination, freeze, deferral Settled #6
Previously

Two earlier ideas: have an autoencoder "discover" the structure the indicator captures; and treat the indicator loosely as a given premise, tuning parameters as needed.

Problem

Autoencoder: a sequence model can represent a path-dependent rule in principle, but a reconstruction objective gives it no incentive to learn that specific predicate, and on this data volume it essentially never will. The loose treatment is worse: the indicator has researcher degrees of freedom baked in — leg definitions, break thresholds, bar frequency were chosen over years of looking at charts where the pattern "worked." It is not merely unvalidated; it is pre-loaded with a bias toward looking good, built to look like it works and now graded on a related exam. The exploration/confirmation machinery only protects against choices made during this project; it does nothing about parameters tuned informally beforehand, including on years inside any holdout. This is genuinely two experiments' worth of validation burden: (1) does regime-conditioning matter (clean; indicator irrelevant), and (2) does this specific indicator carry real out-of-sample information, or was it curve-fit?

Now

Role — Option A, adopted: the indicator is one additional axis, additive, not load-bearing. The regime × fundamentals study is the spine and stands alone; fundamentals enter un-indicator'd and the label is raw future truth, so a dud indicator cannot corrupt the regime finding — the structure axis would simply add nothing (B3 ≈ B2), which is the experiment returning "no," and fine. Its contribution is reported separately and allowed to be negative. (Option B — validate the indicator as its own clean unconditional experiment first — remains the more-work alternative if untangling is ever wanted.) Contamination protocol: (1) freeze the spec in writing before exploration consults forward returns — exactly what a leg is, its origin, the numerical break threshold, the bar frequency; every rule mechanical enough that code, not the eye, makes the call (any rule needing judgment can't be frozen and can't be tested); commit with a timestamp. (2) Run that one frozen version across the entire history and universe with zero per-stock discretion; a genuine mid-project improvement is allowed but demotes its results to exploration-grade. (3) Perturbation check: vary the arbitrary constants (break threshold 0/1/2%, alternate leg definitions) and require the conclusions — sign, which regimes, rough magnitude — to survive; a pattern that exists only at one magic setting is curve-fit. (4) Residual caveat, named in the limitations: the rule was developed over years of charts that can't be un-seen; mitigate with breadth (the most convincing evidence is from the hundreds of names never personally charted) and honest labeling. The forbidden move: quietly re-tuning parameters mid-exploration because "intact wasn't separating returns, let me adjust the pullback threshold" — that is the indicator eating the test set through the back door, the exact p-hacking mechanism of the replication crisis wearing a proprietary badge. Deferral (settled): implementation is postponed; everything else is built and run first. This is architecturally safe — the structure state is just a third column, and Fama-MacBeth doesn't care when it arrives — and arguably the better order: if B1 over B0 is nothing, the spine is dead before any leg-tracking work is sunk. Two things deferral cannot escape: the spec freeze (cheap — one afternoon, do it before exploration starts, because after weeks of seeing the answer landscape no parameter choice can be proven un-nudged even to yourself; cleanliness attaches to when rules were fixed, not when code was written) and the holdout sequencing (the sealed folds can be spent once → OQ1). Bar frequency remains open → OQ2.

Why

Keeps the clean experiment clean, gives the messy one a fair trial, and makes "the indicator failed" a possible result — which is what makes it a real hypothesis rather than a belief.

D13 · Approaches evaluated and rejected (keep these — they earn points)
Now

Reinforcement learning — rejected, two independent reasons: (1) RL assumes stable game rules; market non-stationarity is precisely the rules changing, so RL overfits the old regime and fails when things change, and is harder to debug; (2) RL is catastrophically sample-inefficient and effective N here is tiny — an argument even harder to rebut. Autoencoder discovery of the indicator — rejected (see D12; feed the theory in directly). FAISS + k-NN + XGBoost prediction spine — deleted (D1); simple similarity retrieval survives only as an optional visualization, and if built it must carry the neighbor-pool admissibility rule: a historical point may be shown as an analogue for query t only if its entire label period ended before t (dated ≥ 63 trading days before t) — this leak survives every other guardrail. Retrieval similarity uses within-window normalization (each window standardized on its own, otherwise "similar" degenerates into "same price level / same era"). Transformer encoder — stretch goal with uncertain payoff, not core. "Evaluated and rejected, with reasons" is itself interview material: knowing what not to do demonstrates judgment.

D14 · Point-in-time discipline and drift
Previously

"My data source is clean, so there's no look-ahead problem."

Problem

Look-ahead comes from usage, not source cleanliness. Three risk locations: (a) visibility dates of financials — settled: the fundamentals export is anchored to announcement dates, T+1/T+2, verified by direct probe; (b) restatements — sources tend to carry latest revised figures, not the originals seen at the time (kept as a limitation; usually second-order; optional sensitivity check on restated samples); (c) full-sample leakage inside analysis steps — normalizing 2015 data with statistics through 2024 lets 2015 see the future. Separately, patterns differ across eras for two different reasons needing opposite responses.

Now

Everything is treated as invisible before its release date; macro uses first-release values with computed release dates; all normalizations are either within-day cross-sectional (uses only that day — inherently point-in-time) or trailing-window (uses only the past). Drift A — coordinates moved, not the economy (rates 6% in the 1990s, ~0% in the 2010s): fixed by building regime features as relative quantities — z-scores vs. a trailing window, changes not levels, spreads and curve slopes not raw rates — so "tightening in 1994" and "tightening in 2017" land in the same cluster. Principle: choose features insensitive to what permanently changed but sensitive to what recurs; much apparent "change" is manufactured by wrong coordinates. Drift B — the relationship itself changed ("value works in risk-off" held in the 1990s, reversed after 2008): not fixable, only measurable — slice results by era (2000s / 2010s / 2020s) and state where the pattern held and broke; "held until 2008, then broke" is itself a finding, turning non-stationarity from a problem to hide into a result. Telling them apart: switch to relative features first; residual era differences within the same regimes are Drift B. Corporate actions matter unusually much here: the indicator's core predicate is whether price broke a specific historical level, and a split or large dividend can flip that answer — so price series must be consistently back-adjusted across the full history, verified against known split dates. Staleness: fundamentals are quarterly forward-filled to daily (constant ~98% of days), which distorts clustering and any distance metric if fed naively — handled per OQ3 (cluster on announcement-date snapshots, or carry a days-since-announcement column).

D15 · Engineering hygiene (carried back from the old plan)
Previously

Plan 4 said "reproducible codebase" in one line.

Problem

The concern didn't vanish because the model got simpler. One line is the difference between "engineered" and "vibe-coded."

Now

Restore the old plan's specifics: a leakage unit test asserting no feature at t uses data from after t; modular pipeline; config file + fixed seeds; a logged config-hash → out-of-sample result table so every number is reproducible; a gated build order (data → clusters → statistics → baselines → guardrails → write-up) where each gate has a "done-when" bar — this is what actually protects a summer timeline. All of §3 is organized around these gates.

Phase 2 — Label construction Required · D2 D6

T2.1Point-in-time betas
  1. For each (stock, day t): OLS of daily stock returns on SPY returns over the trailing 252 trading days ending at t−1; require ≥ 126 valid days, else β̂ = 1.
  2. Winsorize β̂ cross-sectionally each day at the 1st/99th percentiles.
  3. Shrink: β* = 0.67·β̂ + 0.33·1 (Blume; Vasicek variance-weighted shrinkage is the alternative).
  4. Store β̂ (pre-shrink) alongside — it is the layer-1 control regressor.
T2.2Beta-residualized rank label
  1. r_fwd(i,t) = adjusted-close return from t to t+63 trading days; r_m = same-window SPY return.
  2. resid(i,t) = r_fwd − β*(i,t)·r_m.
  3. label(i,t) = percentile rank of resid among all universe stocks with valid data that day, in [0,1].
  4. Days with cross-section < min_cross_section: no labels.
Done when: label exists for every eligible (stock, day); sanity check — cross-sectional mean ≈ 0.5 every day by construction; spot-check a few known episodes (e.g., defensive names ranking high in March 2020 residuals).

Phase 3 — Regime clustering Required · D8 D9 D11 D14

T3.1Relative regime features (Drift A by construction)

All features relative, none absolute: SPY trailing 21d/63d returns; 21d realized vol; VIX level as percentile vs. its own trailing 2y; VIX term-structure slope; credit spread (HYG−LQd return spread or a FRED spread series) as level-vs-trailing-z and 21d change; yield-curve slope (10y−2y) and its change; breadth indicators from MKT as trailing z-scores. Trailing windows only — inherently point-in-time.

T3.2Fit, stabilize, freeze
  1. Standardize features using statistics from the initial training window only (2000-07→2011-12, OQ6); fit k-means for k ∈ {4,5,6}.
  2. Stability: block-bootstrap (126d blocks) the training window ~200×, refit, match by centroids, compute adjusted Rand index; pick the k whose partition is most stable and whose clusters are nameable in one line each. Merge/drop unstable clusters.
  3. Freeze: feature spec + scaler stats + centroids saved as the frozen regime model; applied forward unchanged (D8 condition 3).
  4. Unclassified state: compute the 95th percentile of within-cluster distances on the training window; any later day beyond it → "unclassified" (D9). Track the firing rate by year.
  5. Episode segmentation: contiguous same-label runs; runs < 10 trading days → "transition" (excluded from episode counts). Tabulate episodes per regime — this table drives every "sufficient evidence" decision.
Done when: frozen model on disk; each regime named and described; episode table exists; unclassified rate plotted by year.

Phase 5 — Two-layer Fama-MacBeth Required · D5 D6

T5.1Layer 1 — one estimate set per day

For each eligible day t: regress label on financial-state dummies (× structure dummies once the indicator lands) plus β̂ control. Equivalent view for sanity: per-cell mean label minus grand mean. Output: per-day effect vector. Runtime: seconds for the whole history if vectorized.

T5.2Layer 2 — stability and the research question

For each effect series: (a) overall mean with Newey-West SE, 63 lags; (b) era slices (2000s / 2010s / 2020s) — consistency, not just the average (one lucky stretch must not carry the mean); (c) group days by the regime at t and compare mean effects across regimes — the cross-regime difference is the finding; (d) every regime-conditional number carries its episode count, and anything under the D6 threshold prints "insufficient data" instead of a number. Enforced in code, not by discipline.

Done when: a single function produces, for any effect and conditioning, the estimate + NW CI + era slices + episode count + sufficiency verdict.

T7.2Pre-registration & confirmation protocol · D7, replication crisis

4METRICS

What counts as good

No accuracy, no AUC, no Sharpe. The yardstick matches the exploratory framing:

Primary: whether adding conditions robustly changes the return picture — the size of the difference between conditional and unconditional effects, judged by the layer-2 machinery (mean, NW-corrected significance, consistency across eras, episode sufficiency), expressed as the B-increments: how much B1 adds over B0, B2 over B1, B3 over B2.

Secondary (only where stocks genuinely need ranking, e.g., the indicator's stock-selection claim): rank IC (rank correlation between structure state and forward label) and quantile-portfolio return monotonicity — the language quants actually use. Presentational trap: quantile portfolios implicitly invite turnover/cost/capacity questions despite the no-tradability stance. Present quantile returns purely as an effect-size display with that caveat attached, and keep the one-sentence answer ready (§6).

The honesty metric, reported alongside both: the counted-tests statement and the insufficient-data catalogue. "We examined N contrasts, graduated M under pre-registered thresholds, K survived correction on sealed data" is the sentence the entire replication-crisis literature says matters most.

5OPEN

Open questions — decide on purpose, in writing

Nothing here is silently chosen. Each has a default (what the manual assumes if undecided) and alternatives with their costs. Record the resolution in the decision log when made.

OQ1 · Indicator × sealed folds: which sequencing?

The sealed folds can be spent once. If they are opened to confirm B0–B2 before the indicator lands, they can never adjudicate B3 — you've seen the returns.

  • Path A (default): hold confirmation until the indicator is in; pre-register all graduating hypotheses (with and without structure) and open the sealed folds once, at the end. Cost: confirmation waits on R1; if R1 slips, so does confirmation.
  • Path B: confirm B0–B2 on schedule; the indicator's results this summer are exploration-grade, reported as "preliminary, pending out-of-sample confirmation." Cost: weaker indicator claim; benefit: the spine's confirmation is never hostage to R1.
  • The trap is doing neither — spending the holdout early and then quietly reporting indicator results as confirmed.
OQ2 · Indicator bar frequency

The spec must freeze "the bar frequency," singular — but daily/weekly/monthly all exist, and the informal claim is "especially good on weekly/monthly."

  • Freeze one frequency in advance (default: weekly) — cleanest; matches the freeze protocol.
  • Freeze all three as three pre-registered variants — honest, but triples the structure-axis hypothesis count (worsening both the correction budget and the D7 cell collision) and each variant must be counted.
  • Forbidden: run all three, keep the best-looking — exactly the D12 forbidden move.
OQ3 · Financial clustering input & sector treatment
  • Default: announcement-date snapshots, cross-sectional z-scores, days_since_announcement as diagnostic; sector confound stated in write-up.
  • Alternative A: acquire sector mapping (R5) and z-score within sector — dissolves the "rediscovering sectors" confound instead of stating it; cost: mapping acquisition + maintenance.
  • Alternative B: cluster on the daily forward-filled grid with staleness features included — simpler joins, but the clustering is dominated by ~98%-constant values; weakest option.
OQ4 · Banks (7 symbols)
  • Default: exclude from the financial-state axis (remain in universe/label cross-section). Simple, honest, costs 7 of 277 names on one axis.
  • Alternative A: bank-specific features (Net_Income-, EPS-, Net_Interest-based) — requires modifying the cleaning script to restore the dropped Net_Interest columns; adds a special-case pathway.
  • Alternative B: sector-relative features (with OQ3-A) make bank ratios comparable in principle; margin ratios remain undefined where the numerator columns are structurally zero, so A-features are still needed underneath.
OQ5 · Price adjustment status

Unknown until T1.2 runs. Resolutions: already back-adjusted (proceed) · split-adjusted only (proceed; document dividend treatment; keep SPY consistent) · unadjusted (build adjustment factors before Phase 2 — blocking).

OQ6 · Fold boundaries

Default: initial train 2000-07→2011-12 · validation 2012→2019 · sealed 2020→present. Alternative: two separated sealed blocks (e.g., 2013–2015 and 2020→present) for era diversity in confirmation, at the cost of shrinking the validation folds. Either way: committed to config before exploration begins, embargoed at every boundary.

OQ7 · Exploration triage threshold

Which exploration-phase bar graduates a two-way contrast into hypotheses.md? Default: NW t ≥ 2 and effect ≥ 3 percentile points and episode-sufficient. Looser bars graduate more (larger correction penalty at confirmation); stricter bars risk graduating nothing. Fix before exploration ends.

6INTERVIEW

Interview readiness — the hostile-defense sheet

An interview is a hostile thesis defense: the other side hunts for holes. Every answer below is already implemented above — these are retrieval cues, not spin.

"Isn't this just conditional factor models?"

"Yes, deliberately — plus one novel state variable and an unusually honest treatment of effective sample size." Delivered without flinching. The framework is standard (Ferson–Harvey, Hamilton, the practitioner literature); what distinguishes the project is whether every choice survives hostile questioning. Don't sell novelty.

"Is any of this tradable?"

"Out of scope, by design." The project measures whether conditional structure exists and is stable; a tradability claim would require costs, turnover, capacity, and portfolio construction the effective sample can't support — the exact overclaim the framing was chosen to avoid. Quantile returns shown anywhere are effect-size displays, not portfolios.

"What about survivorship bias?"

The signed answer, not the checkbox: "The universe is today's large-cap constituents, so stress-regime and high-leverage cells are upward-biased — the casualties are missing — and the bias grows with lookback distance since early cross-sections are disproportionately future winners. The rank label handles market-level survivorship but not this within-cross-section distortion. Fixing it means CRSP/Sharadar/Norgate or point-in-time index membership; both were considered and declined for scope, and the affected cells are flagged in the report."

"Why only ~200–277 large caps / why start in 2000?"

Universe: intersection of clean technicals and announcement-dated fundamentals; large caps double as the survivorship mitigation. Window: daily market internals begin 2000-06; the window still contains every stress episode the effective-N budget counts on (dot-com bust, GFC, 2011, 2018, COVID, 2022).

"Your regime could change during the 3-month label window."

Conditioning is on the regime at formation date t — "what does the regime now say about what follows" — and regime persistence is part of the hypothesis being measured. Dependence across days is handled by NW errors and episode-level counting, not assumed away.

"What happens when the model sees a market it's never seen?"

It says so: frozen clusters plus a pre-frozen distance threshold route novel days to an explicit "unclassified" state, and the unclassified rate over time is reported as a drift diagnostic — no silent nearest-centroid pretending.

"Why discrete buckets and not a continuous model / gradient boosting?"

The research object is an interaction pinned down by ~4–6 independent episodes per rare regime; a continuous surface has nothing to hold onto, tests uncountably many hypotheses, and has no clean stability check. Discrete as primary because effective N demands it and interpretability is the deliverable; continuous interactions run as a robustness check — an effect that vanishes continuously was a bin-boundary artifact. Never claim continuous is impossible.

"Why no deep learning? Why did you reject RL?"

No training runs exist in the core by design. RL: non-stationarity violates its stable-rules premise, and it is catastrophically sample-inefficient against a tiny effective N — two independent reasons. Autoencoder: a reconstruction objective has no incentive to learn a path-dependent predicate on this data volume; the theory is fed in directly instead.

"How do I know your indicator isn't curve-fit hindsight?"

"It might be — that's why it's a hypothesis, not a premise." Spec frozen in writing before exploration; run mechanically over the full history with zero discretion; perturbation-checked at its arbitrary constants; graded on its increment over regime + fundamentals (B3−B2), which is allowed to be zero; and the residual contamination — years of charts that can't be un-seen — is named in the limitations, mitigated by breadth across never-charted names.

"How many things did you test?"

Exact counts, pre-registered graduation thresholds, stated correction, and the insufficient-data catalogue — the direct application of the replication-crisis lesson (64–85% of published anomalies failing consistent replication is why this sentence exists).

"And if you find nothing?"

"Then the finding is that nothing survived honest correction — which is a defensible research result here, and would just be a broken machine under the prediction framing. That asymmetry is why the framing was chosen."

7RUNTIME

Runtime & effort budget

Bottlenecked by thinking and data-wrangling, not machine time — a feature, but it also means "it's still running" is rarely a valid excuse.

No neural-network training exists in the core, so there are no long training runs. A genuinely long-running core computation signals un-vectorized code, not a big problem — at this data size there isn't one. Guard iteration speed: a 40-minute loop waited on 15×/day is the real cost.

8DELIVER

Deliverables & signed limitations

Deliverables

  • Reproducible codebase: visibility-enforcing data layer, frozen cluster models, two-layer statistics, B0–B3, the rigor suite, leakage tests in CI, config + seeds + results log.
  • The report (T9.1), including the incremental-value tables, the confirmation outcome, and the insufficient-data catalogue.
  • Named, described clusters and a few illustrated conditional patterns.
  • indicator_spec.md (timestamped), hypotheses.md (pre-registered), the decision log resolving OQ1–OQ7.

Limitations — signed, not just named

  • Survivorship: current-constituent universe ⇒ stress-regime and high-leverage cells upward-biased; bias grows with lookback. Fix known and declined for scope (D3).
  • Regime axis is inherently small-sample: a physical ceiling — clustering reduces dimensionality but cannot conjure independent episodes; conclusions there are small-N by nature.
  • Restatements: visibility dates are announcement-anchored (settled), but some values may be later-revised figures rather than as-first-seen; second-order; sensitivity check optional (R4).
  • Macro release dates are calculated approximations, not archival publication timestamps.
  • Non-stationarity: half circumvented via relative features (Drift A), half measured and reported by era (Drift B); the irreducible remainder acknowledged.
  • Indicator provenance: developed on charts that overlap the evaluation years; the freeze + perturbation + breadth protocol bounds but cannot erase this (D12).
  • Generality: US large caps, 2000→present, one horizon; findings are claims about that world.
Why volunteer all this

An honest researcher states the premises under which a conclusion holds. That is not weakness — it is precisely what makes the other conclusions trustworthy, and it is the quality interviewers screen for.
